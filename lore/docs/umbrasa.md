The Umbrasa, often shortened to Umbra, are a people descended from the labor crews who once mined helium-3 beneath the surface of Ilyr's moon. The Undertow destroyed the world those workers knew, shattered the moon and left its fragments held around the black hole they call Hollmaw. Those who survived did so through the Stillness, a long, motionless suspension in fluid helium that changed their bodies and bound them to the equipment that had kept them alive.

Compact and sturdy, the Umbrasa carry their ancestors' labor visibly in their flesh. Mining exoskeletons fused into muscle and bone, becoming protruding metallic plates rather than armor that can be removed. Their porous bones participate in helium respiration, while their exhaled neon becomes luminous in electrically charged air. Elongated, bat-like ears accompany a perception of gravity that extends through the body, allowing them to feel pressures and fluctuations that other peoples recognize only when something begins to fall.

Their civilization grew among the broken lunar fragments of Muunbrak, a name descended from Moonbreak. Life there demanded an understanding of weight, shelter and the invisible pulls between pieces of a ruined world. Pathrunners learned to read those pulls as routes, carrying messages and supplies between separated communities. Later generations reached Ilyr, where Umbrasa mountain strongholds became part of the world's shared history.

The Tide brought another upheaval to a people already shaped by catastrophe. The Drowning unsettled the ground and routes on which they depended; the Divergence deepened their relationship with gravity; and the Drift carried unfamiliar objects and histories into their homes. Today the Umbrasa live between lunar settlements and communities on Ilyr, preserving a culture in which practical knowledge, mutual dependence and the wonder of light in darkness remain closely joined.

## Origins and Early Evolution

### The Crews Before the Undertow

Before they were Umbrasa, they were humans working far from home. They had traveled to the moon on helium-3 contracts, entering mines and processing works that supplied the energy demands of the world below. Many had families on Earth and reasons for enduring the distance: wages sent home, obligations waiting at the end of a contract, and the hope that years of difficult work might secure a better life.

Their settlements were places built around labor. Machinery, pressure systems and protective equipment stood between the crews and an environment that could not support them unaided. Their working bodies were extended by mining rigs and exoskeletons, while their survival depended on the reliability of systems most people on Earth never needed to think about.

The habits that later shaped Umbrasa society began here. Workers learned to judge the condition of a passage, notice a change in pressure and trust the person checking a seal. Those ordinary acts of attention became more important than the institutions that had sent them to the moon. When those institutions disappeared, the knowledge of the crews remained.

### Muunbrak and Hollmaw

The Undertow reached beyond Earth's surface. On the moon, its effects broke apart the landscape that held the mines and their settlements. The moon shattered, and its fragments were drawn back toward Hollmaw, a black hole around which the broken pieces became pinned. Muunbrak was the name that eventually grew from Moonbreak, preserving the memory of the disaster in the speech of its survivors.

The fragments were not simply a ring of ordinary moons following familiar orbits. They formed a broken arrangement held in the influence of Hollmaw. What had once been a connected lunar surface became separated masses of rock, ruined workings and enclosed spaces. The survivors' future depended on learning what could remain stable within that arrangement.

The earliest crews had no opportunity to understand the whole event. Their immediate world contracted to the spaces that still held them and the equipment still surrounding their bodies. The larger history of Earth slipped beyond their reach while another, stranger transformation began within the mines.

### The Stillness

For roughly thirty years, the surviving workers remained in the Stillness. Suspended in fluid helium, they lay motionless while their bodies changed. It was an eerie interval of apparent inactivity, with the old mining equipment held against them as flesh, bone and machinery gradually became inseparable.

The rigs did not become possessions they could discard upon waking. They became part of the body itself, their supports and surfaces incorporated into living structure. Metal emerged as visible skin-plates, and the workers' internal anatomy changed around a dependence on helium. Their bones grew porous and took on a role in respiration that their human ancestors had never possessed.

When the Stillness ended, the survivors retained memories of having been human. That continuity distinguished their experience from peoples whose transformations erased much of their personal past. Yet memory offered no route back to the lives they had lost. Their families, contracts and familiar society belonged to a world that had vanished or moved beyond them.

The name Umbrasa grew from Umbra, the protective shadow in which their surviving lives had taken shape. What began as the aftermath of a disaster became an identity carried by those who awoke and by the generations that followed them.

## The Age of the Abyssal Veil

### Learning the Broken Moon

The Umbrasa's first society formed within the spaces left to them. Broken workings and sheltered portions of lunar fragments became the beginnings of settlements. Their bodies could endure conditions their ancestors had required equipment to survive, but that change did not make Muunbrak harmless. Helium, pressure and gravity remained the terms on which life was possible.

They learned their environment partly through sensation. A change in the pull through a corridor or beneath a standing body could signal a condition that sight did not reveal. Pullsense became the everyday language of this awareness. The Umbrasa did not need to describe gravity as an abstraction before they could notice it pressing differently against them.

Their practical vocabulary followed the same pattern. A skin-plate was part of the body; bone-breathing described what sustained it; pull-lines described the paths of force that could be read and followed. Their terms grew from what people did, felt and needed to communicate. The language of the old crews survived in those direct compounds even as the world around them became unfamiliar.

### Pathrunners and Separated Settlements

Isolation made communication a difficult and valuable undertaking. Pathrunners became the couriers who could interpret gravitational conditions and travel between separated communities. A route was more than a line between two places. It depended on the forces along that line, the conditions at its ends and the traveler's ability to recognize when those conditions had changed.

Through those journeys, messages, supplies and accounts of surviving settlements could move beyond individual fragments. The Pathrunners helped turn isolated groups into a wider people. Their importance came from a skill rooted in Umbrasa perception, strengthened through experience and used in service of others who could not make the same journeys safely.

### Weight and Wonder

Life among the fragments gave weight an immediate cultural meaning. It could be trusted, misjudged, shared or lost. Balance was a condition people worked to maintain in their surroundings and in their relationships, rather than an idea separated from daily survival.

Alongside that practical understanding grew an appreciation for a phenomenon their bodies carried into the air. Neon exhaled through their altered respiration became visible during electrical storms and static discharges. Under those conditions, breath and movement could leave brief luminous traces. The light was conditional, appearing when the surrounding air allowed it, rather than a constant glow produced at will.

Neon performances developed from this meeting of body and environment. For a people who lived with the weight of a broken home, those moments offered a visible form of wonder. The expression Weight and Wonder brings together the two concerns without separating the work of survival from the experiences that made survival worth preserving.

## Reconnection and the Era of Verdancy

Over time, the Umbrasa developed the means to travel beyond the confines of Muunbrak. Star-Barges and Helio-Regulators made movement between their helium-dependent lives and the environments of Ilyr possible. Their return was a migration by a changed people toward a planet that had also changed beyond recognition.

They did not return as the same contract workers who had left Earth. Their bodies could no longer treat the planet's air as something taken for granted, and much of the world their ancestors remembered had disappeared. Reconnection therefore required technological support as well as the work of finding a place among other societies.

Ilyr's shared histories record the Teruānga encountering Umbrasa mountain strongholds during the widening contact of the Era of Verdancy. These settlements belong to the Umbrasa story alongside the communities of the lunar fragments. Their existence reflects a people whose history reaches beyond one environment, even while their anatomy continues to carry the conditions of their transformation.

Contact with other peoples widened their place in the world. The skills that had maintained routes among lunar settlements could also support exchange and diplomacy. At the same time, the difference between lunar life and life on Ilyr ensured that returning to the planet did not end their attachment to Muunbrak.

## Impact of the Tide's Three Consequences

### The Drowning

The Umbrasa had already inherited a shattered home from the Undertow when the later disruptions of the Tide threatened the arrangements on which they depended. The Drowning made familiar ground and connections unreliable. For communities whose survival rested on reading weight and maintaining access between fragments, instability could break the practical ties holding a settlement together.

Shard-caravans became an expression of survival through movement. Communities carried what they could preserve when remaining in place could no longer be trusted. The response drew on an old Umbrasa necessity: a home had to be understood through the conditions that sustained it, and those conditions could change.

### The Divergence

The Divergence intensified abilities already rooted in Umbrasa anatomy and experience. Their sensitivity to gravity developed into a broader range of gravipathic powers, with some individuals able to do more than perceive the forces passing through and around them.

Among the changed were weight-shifters, whose relationship with bodily mass, pressure and gravitational effects went beyond ordinary pullsense. Their emergence altered what movement and labor could mean within Umbrasa communities. The same force that had defined the limits of a journey could, in some hands, become something actively influenced.

These gifts were not shared identically by every Umbrasa. The distinction between the widespread sensory adaptation and the more exceptional powers of particular individuals remained important. A people accustomed to feeling gravity did not become a society in which everyone commanded it without limit.

### The Drift

The Drift brought a different uncertainty. Objects, places and fragments of other histories could appear within an environment the Umbrasa already worked hard to understand. The unfamiliar was no longer limited to distant settlements or the changed peoples of Ilyr; it could enter the routes and spaces of their own lives.

Echo-houses became associated with the traces and anomalies carried by these arrivals. The Drift gave the Umbrasa encounters with histories outside their own continuity, deepening the tension between what memory preserved and what reality could unexpectedly place before them.

## The Umbrasa Today

The Umbrasa now live across lunar settlements and communities on Ilyr. Mountain strongholds connect them to the planet's recorded history, while the fragments of Muunbrak remain central to their origins. Travel, exchange and pilgrimage sustain relationships between those places rather than reducing their identity to a single homeland.

Their bodies make that shared identity visible. An Umbrasa on Ilyr still carries skin-plates descended from the rigs of the first crews and still depends on the respiratory conditions created by the Stillness. Technology helps them move between environments, but it does not erase the history embodied in them.

The Tide has complicated their future without displacing the concerns that shaped their beginnings. They must maintain places where they can breathe, routes by which people can reach one another and practices through which knowledge survives. Within those demands, their performances and gatherings preserve an appreciation for the brief, luminous moments that their ancestors first found in the darkness of a broken moon.

## Anatomy

The Umbrasa have compact, sturdy frames. Their fused exoskeletons contribute to an appearance in which living tissue and metallic structure meet visibly. The protruding plates are incorporated into their anatomy, rather than forming a removable outer suit.

Their bones are porous and participate in helium uptake, giving rise to the term bone-breathing. This respiratory dependence distinguishes them from humans and makes environmental support necessary when they live or travel outside suitable helium conditions. Helio-Regulators enable that adaptation to places such as Ilyr.

Neon is exhaled as part of their altered respiration. Its characteristic light appears when electrical storms or static discharges energize the surrounding gas. An Umbrasa standing in ordinary, uncharged air does not constantly trail visible neon.

Elongated, bat-like ears are another recognizable feature. Their gravipathic perception, however, is described through the body as a whole. Pullsense makes gravitational pressure and fluctuation part of their immediate awareness, complementing the senses through which other peoples navigate.

## Behavior

The Umbrasa's history favors attention to conditions that can change beneath apparent stability. Their expertise grows from noticing, interpreting and responding to those changes. Caution in a dangerous passage or respect for a carefully maintained route follows from a practical understanding of what failure can cost.

Their inherited working culture places value on useful knowledge and the people who carry it. Pathrunners embody one expression of that value, linking separated communities through an ability developed by repeated experience. The importance of such roles comes from the relationships they sustain as much as from individual skill.

These habits do not make all Umbrasa alike. Communities on Ilyr and settlements among Muunbrak's fragments face different pressures, while the Tide has changed individual capabilities unevenly. Their shared history supplies a foundation for identity without fixing every person's temperament or ambitions.

## Unique Abilities

### Pullsense

Pullsense is the Umbrasa awareness of gravitational pressure and fluctuation. It allows them to recognize conditions that may not be visible, making it essential to movement through their unusual environments. It is a sensory relationship with gravity, distinct from the ability to alter it.

### Reading Pull Lines

Experienced travelers interpret the patterns of gravitational influence along a route. Pathrunners develop this knowledge into a demanding practical skill, using it to connect places separated by the broken geography of their home. Knowing a route includes understanding when it is no longer safe to follow.

### Weight Shifting

The Divergence opened more active gravitational abilities to some Umbrasa. Weight-shifters represent this development, extending the people's older sensitivity into manipulation. These exceptional capacities remain distinct from the adaptations shared throughout the population.

### Neon Breath

Neon-breath becomes visible under electrical excitation. Although it supports performances and recognizable trails in charged air, its illumination depends on environmental conditions. It is a consequence of Umbrasa physiology that has acquired cultural meaning, rather than an unrestricted ability to create light.

## Culture and Community

Umbrasa culture retains the language and practical inheritance of labor crews whose survival depended on one another. The meanings of their common terms remain close to the body, the task and the place: skin-plates, bone-breathing and pull-lines name things a person can encounter directly.

Memory also carries a particular weight. The first survivors remembered having been human, even as their own bodies made that former life impossible to resume. Their descendants inherit a history in which the past is neither wholly forgotten nor physically recoverable. Muunbrak preserves that tension as both a name for home and a name born from its breaking.

Neon gatherings and performances offer a communal expression of their altered lives. The conditions that reveal their breath can transform a familiar space into one traced with light. Such occasions sit alongside the maintenance of settlements, the work of travel and the journeys through which lunar and planetary communities remain connected.

## Habitat and Location

The Umbrasa's ancestral habitat lies among the fragments of Muunbrak, held in the influence of Hollmaw. Their settlements grew from the environments in which the Stillness left them and from their developing ability to understand helium conditions, pressure and gravitational forces.

On Ilyr, mountain strongholds are explicitly remembered in the accounts of Teruānga exploration. Floating-island and mountain communities also suit their wider association with difficult gravitational environments, although living on the planet continues to require support for helium respiration.

Star-Barges and Helio-Regulators connect these settings technologically. Journeys between them allow trade, contact and pilgrimage, maintaining the relationship between a people living on Ilyr and the broken lunar home from which they emerged.

## Details Still to Be Defined

The Umbrasa's reproductive biology, gestation, childhood development and typical lifespan have not yet been fixed. Their final account should establish how growing bodies develop their plates and respiratory structures, and what environmental support is required during early life.

The exact chronology of individual lunar fragment movements during the later Drowning also remains to be set. The Undertow's original destruction of the moon and the Tide's subsequent disruption should remain separate events in that history.

Names, institutions and internal divisions beyond the established roles and communities remain open for further development. These questions leave room to deepen Umbrasa society without replacing the origins, anatomy and language already attached to them.
