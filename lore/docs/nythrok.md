The **Nyth’rok** are a race transformed by the **Tide**, a cataclysmic event on **Primus** that left them irrevocably altered. Originally descended from Nordic humans, they were forever changed by the **Veigrstorm**, a quantum storm that fused their bodies and minds with glimpses of alternate realities, elongating their forms and imbuing them with haunting memories of lives unlived. This transformation led to their foundational philosophy, **Dýprót**, or the **Deep Root**, which emphasizes that each choice sends roots deeper into reality, strengthening their place in the world. Guided by this belief, the Nyth’rok evolved culturally and physically, branching into distinct **factions**, each interpreting Dýprót in its own way.

The **Nyth’vael** embody tradition and preservation, residing in the ghostly **Stillholds** where they guard the **Rótbook**, their ancient record of history and philosophy. The nomadic **Nyth’vyr**, resembling their Viking ancestors in build and spirit, embrace resilience and adaptability, roaming Primus as warriors and explorers. The scholarly **Boundless**, or **Nyth’dar**, delve into the mysteries of time and existence, piecing together fragments of lost knowledge and studying quantum powers. Lastly, the isolationist **Nyth’rundr**, a subset of the Nyth’vael, reside in temporal **Aeternums**, perfecting themselves through genetic modification and strict seclusion.

Through **The Divergence**, the Nyth’rok gained mastery over quantum powers: **Chronomancy** (time manipulation), **Superposition** (probabilistic abilities), and **Entanglement** (instantaneous connection). These powers have altered the dynamics within their society, deepening divisions and reordering alliances. **The Drift**—the third consequence of the Tide—introduced enclaves from distant worlds, which the Nyth’rok found themselves strangely familiar with, able to intuitively grasp their languages and customs as if through ancestral memory. The Boundless study these enclaves in pursuit of historical truths, while the Nyth’vyr serve as intermediaries, bridging connections across Primus.

Now, the Nyth’rok stand at a crossroads, each faction pursuing its own vision of the future in a fractured society. Their mastery of quantum forces and the philosophy of Dýprót guide them, binding them to their past while pulling them toward a future where they shape their world and their fates in equal measure. Resilient and complex, the Nyth’rok are both guardians of tradition and seekers of new truths, a race profoundly aware of the universe’s hidden paths.

## Origins and Early Evolution (Post-Undertow)

The origins of the Nyth’rok are written in the scars left by a single, catastrophic event that would come to be known as the *Veigrstorm*. Named after the ancient Norse word for "way" or "path," this storm was no mere tempest. It was a cosmic anomaly, a quantum storm carrying with it the strange and unpredictable energies of the Undertow—a force that seemed to bend reality itself. The name *Veigrstorm* was given by the few scholars and elders who remembered the old word, believing this unnatural storm was not simply a destructive event, but a new “path” thrust upon their people, one that would forever alter the course of their existence.

The Veigrstorm struck the northern lands, a region already known for its brutal weather, unforgiving fjords, and towering mountains. The people there had lived for centuries in harmony with the elements, accustomed to the fierce winter winds and the heavy storms that roared over the sea. But the Veigrstorm was different. Those who survived its passing would later say it was as though reality itself began to come undone, as if the laws that held the world together were slipping.

To understand what the Nyth’rok endured, it helps to know something of the phenomenon known as *quantum superposition*. In the simplest terms, superposition describes a state in which multiple realities or possibilities coexist at once, a concept seen in quantum physics where a particle can exist in multiple states simultaneously until observed or measured. In the case of the Veigrstorm, this principle wasn’t merely theoretical. The storm forced those within its reach to experience these multiple states, casting them into a shifting, chaotic state of being where countless possible versions of themselves and their world flickered in and out of existence.

When the eye of the Veigrstorm passed directly over them, they felt reality split around them. One heartbeat, they stood as themselves, whole and solid; the next, they glimpsed twisted, impossible forms—stretched limbs, elongated faces, strange silhouettes half-seen in flickering flashes of lightning. In one moment, they were humans in their homes; in the next, they found themselves as entirely different beings, caught in a maelstrom of quantum possibilities, each one as real as the last. This experience was described as disorienting, as if they were “walking between worlds,” each step leading them to a new self, a new life, a new reality.

The Veigrstorm didn’t merely twist their perceptions. It warped their physical forms. Over the hours and days that the storm lingered, its energies tugged at their bodies, reshaping them in grotesque, surreal ways. Limbs grew long and thin, faces stretched, ears warped upward to bat-like points, as though their bodies were trying to adapt to the strange forces that surrounded them. This was not a transformation by choice or design; it was an adaptation forced upon them by the very fabric of their environment, an instinctual response to the quantum forces that tore at them from within and without.

As the storm finally passed and the strange energies of the Veigrstorm ebbed, the people were left changed—not just physically, but mentally. The constant, overlapping realities had overburdened their minds, flooding them with memories of lives they had never truly lived. Their consciousnesses buckled under the weight, unable to process the barrage of countless versions of themselves. In a final, desperate attempt at survival, their minds seemed to reset, shedding all memories of their former lives. Personal histories, families, identities—everything was erased in an instant, leaving behind only a haunting sense of what once was, vague impressions of selves they could no longer fully grasp.

The world they awoke to was just as alien. The Veigrstorm had left the northern landscape strewn with detritus from other realms—twisted metal, shattered machinery, and the remains of elements and materials never before seen in this universe. The land had become a graveyard of alien wreckage, as if the storm had reached through the boundaries of reality, pulling pieces from countless worlds and scattering them across the fjords and mountains. None of these remnants were whole, and nothing of use could be salvaged; it was as if the storm had shredded them in transit, leaving only broken fragments of machinery and metals that hummed faintly with unnatural energies. What should have been recognizable tools and technologies were instead reduced to unrecognizable junk, twisted and warped beyond repair, a junkyard of fractured realities.

In the years following the Veigrstorm, the newly formed Nyth’rok—creatures of elongated limbs and sharp, haunting features—tried to rebuild their lives amid this wasteland. Although they had lost all memory of who they had been, they retained a quiet resilience, born from centuries of enduring the north’s harsh landscapes. They found that their senses had been sharpened slightly; their elongated ears granted them acute hearing, and their vision had adjusted to better perceive movement and detail in the dim light of their devastated homeland. These changes were minor but gave them a faint edge in surviving the ruined lands.

For generations, the Nyth’rok would share tales of the Veigrstorm and the path it had carved through their history. They spoke of it as both curse and origin, a storm that had taken everything from them yet had given them a strange new existence. In time, the Nyth’rok would come to see themselves as creatures born from the storm, a people shaped by quantum chaos, bound to the debris and strange energies left behind.

Thus, the Nyth’rok entered a new era—haunted by ghostly memories of lives they had never lived, scattered across a land littered with the remains of other realities, forever tied to the Veigrstorm that had carved a new path through their world and forever altered their fate.

## The Nyth’rok in the Age of the Abyssal Veil

In the years following the Veigrstorm, the Nyth’rok emerged from the wreckage of their homeland as a changed people. Physically and mentally reshaped by the storm’s quantum chaos, they bore the scars of an event that had torn through the very fabric of their existence. They were haunted by fractured memories and glimpses of lives they had never lived, as if parts of their consciousness had been momentarily thrown into other realities. During the Veigrstorm, they had experienced superposition—a state where countless alternate versions of themselves had flickered in and out of being. This left them with a faint awareness of the possibilities that lay beyond the boundaries of their current reality, like dreams barely remembered but forever lingering at the edges of their minds.

As they began to sift through the physical remnants of the storm—alien metals, unearthly elements, and strange fragments from other realms—they also confronted the existential remnants it had left within them. They found scattered symbols and artifacts from their forgotten Nordic roots, echoes of an ancient identity that had been partially erased by the Veigrstorm. These remnants became anchors in a turbulent sea of uncertainty, helping them piece together a new sense of self. Out of this process of reconstruction came a guiding philosophy to make sense of their altered reality: *Dýprót*—*The Deep Root*.

*Dýprót* was, in part, inspired by half-remembered tales of the World Tree, a powerful symbol they found recurring in fragments of their lost culture. In ancient myths, the World Tree was said to hold many worlds among its branches—each world distinct, yet connected to the others through a vast, shared root system. To the Nyth’rok, this idea resonated deeply. They saw a parallel between the World Tree and the realities they had brushed against during the Veigrstorm, as if the storm had briefly revealed to them a cosmic network of possibilities rooted in the same origin. *Dýprót* became an allusion to this concept: a belief that their choices were roots digging into the cosmic soil, and that by growing deep and strong, they could support an expansive future, branching into countless possible paths just as the World Tree held its many worlds.

This newfound philosophy transformed the Nyth’rok. Through *Dýprót*, they embraced the idea that every decision was a root extending into the fabric of reality, each choice creating a foundation upon which future branches could grow. They came to believe that their strength as a society would depend on the depth of these roots, built on the wisdom and foresight of their foundational choices. In their eyes, impulsive decisions were shallow roots—weak, vulnerable, and unlikely to sustain their civilization in the long term. By contrast, thoughtful, deliberate choices would lay strong roots, empowering their people to adapt, grow, and reach toward new horizons.

This belief fostered a culture of profound caution and careful decision-making. Every choice was weighed for its long-term impact, its potential to either nourish or destabilize the whole. As each generation passed, the Nyth’rok sought to dig their roots deeper, anchoring themselves more firmly in the soil of their shared history and collective wisdom. This strong foundation allowed them to survive the post-storm chaos, creating a stable society that could endure, grow, and thrive in an uncertain world.

### The Rótbook and the Rise of the Nyth’vael

In the aftermath of the Veigrstorm, as the Nyth’rok sought to organize their fragmented memories and newly-formed philosophies, they established *The Rótbook*—a central archive that would become the soul of their society. The Rótbook was not merely a record of events or a history book; it was the embodiment of *Dýprót*, a living document that collected the deep-rooted choices and philosophies that would guide all future generations. Each entry in the Rótbook was treated with reverence, every decision meticulously recorded and carefully preserved. To the Nyth’rok, each documented choice was a root embedded in the vast tree of their culture, providing stability and continuity in a world forever changed by the Veigrstorm.

The Rótbook was more than just a collection of memories; it was a symbol of their identity, an unbroken chain that linked them to their ancestors and the foundational principles of *Dýprót*. Decisions were not made lightly, and every entry was crafted with the awareness that it would set precedent, creating pathways for generations to come. The Rótbook became a sacred text, consulted by leaders, scholars, and philosophers alike to ensure that every choice they made was rooted in the core values of their people. Through the Rótbook, the Nyth’rok established a sense of continuity, grounding themselves in a coherent narrative that provided guidance and stability.

From the reverence for the Rótbook grew a hierarchy of interpretations around *Dýprót*, each faction offering its own view of how best to embody the teachings preserved within its pages. While various factions emerged over time, each with a unique perspective, it was the *Nyth’vael* who ultimately became the dominant force. The Nyth’vael were not merely a faction; they were the heart of Nyth’rok society, embodying both the majority of the population and the cultural leadership of their people. They positioned themselves as the custodians of *The Deep Root*, taking on the responsibility of upholding the continuity and stability of their civilization.

To the Nyth’vael, *Dýprót* demanded careful control, preservation, and a strict adherence to the choices documented in the Rótbook. They believed that the strength of the Nyth’rok lay in safeguarding these foundational decisions and maintaining a stable society, free from the chaotic influences of the unknown. For the Nyth’vael, *Dýprót* was a mandate for rooted tradition, an unbroken line that stretched from the past to the present and onward into the future. This meant that innovation was approached with caution; only those advancements that reinforced their values and served the community in structured, predictable ways were permitted.

The Nyth’vael built their society around a strict regimen of ritual and structure. They emphasized the importance of cycles—periods of work, rest, and remembrance—each one designed to keep the community in harmony with *The Deep Root*. Rituals were held to honor the choices recorded in the Rótbook, gatherings where the community would reflect on the wisdom of the past and renew their commitment to stability. To the Nyth’vael, every action was part of a larger cycle, reinforcing the roots of their society and ensuring that each generation would add to the strength of the last.

In keeping with their cautious philosophy, the Nyth’vael approached technology as a means of refinement rather than exploration. While they were not opposed to innovation, every new tool or advancement was scrutinized for its potential impact on the stability of their society. The Nyth’vael excelled in infrastructure, sustainable resource management, and efficient design, creating systems that were durable, reliable, and in harmony with the environment. Their advances were subtle but effective, focused on longevity and resilience rather than rapid change. They saw technology as a tool to strengthen their roots, to make their foundation unshakable rather than to venture into unknown realms.

Physically, the Nyth’vael embodied the calm, disciplined lifestyle that defined their philosophy. Their tall, statuesque forms, hairless heads, and narrow, elongated features lent them an ethereal, almost otherworldly air. Their movements were precise and deliberate, their expressions calm and composed. The Nyth’vael cultivated an aesthetic of restrained elegance, often dressing in minimalistic attire adorned with subtle symbols that echoed their lost Nordic heritage. To outsiders, they appeared serene, aloof, and unshakeable, as if they themselves were rooted deeply in the ground beneath them.

The Nyth’vael saw themselves as the “trunk” of Nyth’rok civilization, holding it steady as the branches above grew in controlled, purposeful directions. To them, the Rótbook was more than a tool; it was a beacon, guiding the community toward a future that honored the past. They viewed themselves as the keepers of the Rótbook’s legacy, and as such, they believed that their understanding of *Dýprót* was the truest and most essential. In their eyes, those who strayed from the Rótbook’s teachings were weakening the roots of the Nyth’rok, endangering the stability they had worked so hard to build.

In their role as the guardians of *The Deep Root*, the Nyth’vael exercised immense influence over the other factions. They enforced a strict social order, with the Rótbook serving as both a moral and legal guide. This hierarchical structure cemented the Nyth’vael’s authority, as their interpretations of *Dýprót* became the standards by which all others were judged. Although other factions held differing views on how to embody the philosophy of *The Deep Root*, the Nyth’vael’s control over the Rótbook ensured that their interpretation remained dominant.

Through the Rótbook and their disciplined adherence to tradition, the Nyth’vael cultivated a society that was stable, enduring, and deeply rooted in continuity. They believed that by remaining firmly anchored to the past, they could ensure a future that would withstand any storm. For the Nyth’vael, the legacy of *The Deep Root* was not merely to survive—it was to create a civilization so deeply entrenched in wisdom and stability that it would endure for generations, unshaken and unwavering.

### The Nyth’vyr: The Unrooted

In stark contrast to the Nyth’vael’s structured and deeply rooted approach, the Nyth’vyr emerged not as a formal faction but as a way of life—a solitary path chosen by those who saw *Dýprót* as a call to expand outward, rather than to grow solely within. While the Nyth’vael believed that strength came from cultivating roots in a single, carefully tended homeland, the Nyth’vyr held a different view. To them, *The Deep Root* required spreading across many soils, gathering strength from diverse environments. They believed that if they confined themselves to the same land as the Nyth’vael, the ground beneath them would grow barren, unable to nourish the full potential of their people. To the Nyth’vyr, true depth came from embedding roots in many places, absorbing strength from the richness of varied experiences.

For the Nyth’vyr, *Dýprót* meant that roots must seek fresh, fertile soil—other lands, other cultures, new perspectives. Each place they traveled to was an opportunity to plant themselves, however briefly, and absorb resilience from their surroundings. This drive led the Nyth’vyr to a life of wandering, each one journeying alone, rarely—if ever—encountering others of their kind. Strength, they believed, came from individuality and from pushing their roots into unfamiliar ground. In their view, the vitality of *The Deep Root* lay not in staying put but in spreading far.

The Nyth’vael, however, saw this philosophy as a betrayal of *Dýprót*. To them, the Nyth’vyr were not merely wanderers—they were *Unrooted*, an insult that conveyed their lack of grounding and perceived superficiality. The Nyth’vael believed that true strength required collective roots, deeply embedded in a single, cultivated homeland. In their eyes, the Nyth’vyr’s restless movement and refusal to settle in one place made them weak, untethered from the source of their people’s strength. To be *Unrooted* was to be shallow, without foundation, scattered across the world rather than firmly planted within it.

The Nyth’vyr understood the insult embedded in the term, but they chose to embrace it. Though they did not agree with the Nyth’vael’s interpretation, they took on the name *Unrooted* with a quiet pride. At their core, the Nyth’vyr knew that their understanding of *The Deep Root* was no less strong, and perhaps stronger. They believed that their way of life allowed them to embody *Dýprót* in its fullest form, drawing strength from soils the Nyth’vael could never touch. To the Nyth’vyr, the label of *Unrooted* was merely a word; they knew that their roots were deep, extending across vast and varied lands, each one enriching the whole.

Effectively exiled by the Nyth’vael, the Nyth’vyr rarely returned home. Though no law barred them, the social and cultural divide made returning an uncomfortable prospect. Few ever sought to rejoin the society that saw them as outcasts. Instead, they found purpose in their exile, carving out identities as wanderers and explorers, bringing the strength of *Dýprót* to new lands and new experiences. For them, exile was not a punishment but a calling, a different interpretation of *The Deep Root* that led them to seek strength in solitude and resilience in unfamiliar soil.

When they appeared in distant lands, hired as mercenaries, scouts, or advisors, they always traveled alone—enigmatic figures, self-sufficient and silent, never seen in groups. The Nyth’vyr became known across continents as adaptable, resilient warriors, carrying with them the quiet strength of those who had made peace with solitude. To outsiders, the Nyth’vyr were mysterious and solitary, embodying a unique power that spoke of roots hidden and spread across lands unseen—a hidden depth only they understood.

In this way, the Nyth’vyr became the *Unrooted*—an offshoot of the Nyth’rok culture that had drifted from the core, each individual planting themselves in foreign lands, gathering strength from the diversity of the world, yet always carrying the memory of a home that had cast them out. They wore the title *Unrooted* as a badge, knowing that their understanding of *The Deep Root* was one of quiet resilience, drawing strength from wherever they roamed, and confident that their roots ran deeper than others could see.

### Technological Growth Rooted in Dýprót

The differing philosophies of the Nyth’vael and the Nyth’vyr led to a unique duality in the Nyth’rok’s approach to technology. United by *Dýprót*, the Nyth’rok saw technology as both a root and a branch—a tool that could deepen their foundations while also allowing them to reach for new possibilities. The raw materials left behind by the Veigrstorm provided the resources, but *The Deep Root* guided how they would be used.

The Nyth’vael embraced technology as a means of reinforcing stability. Every innovation was carefully scrutinized and designed to enhance efficiency, longevity, and self-sufficiency within their controlled society. Their technological advances were conservative but highly effective, resulting in infrastructure and tools that were finely tuned to the needs of their community. They excelled in sustainable resource management, architectural refinement, and creating a balanced ecosystem within their homeland. To the Nyth’vael, technology was not a way to reach outward, but to strengthen their roots in the soil they had carefully cultivated.

For the Nyth’vyr, technology was an adaptive tool, one that allowed them to thrive in the ever-changing environments they encountered. They became experts in survival technology, developing lightweight, versatile gear suited for travel and exploration. They scavenged from other cultures, incorporating foreign advancements and ideas into their own tools. This approach made the Nyth’vyr’s technology eclectic and improvisational, yet effective in ways the Nyth’vael’s structured innovations could not match. Each piece of equipment they carried was a testament to their belief in roots that could grow across many lands, adapting and absorbing strength from each place they touched.

Together, the Nyth’vael and Nyth’vyr created a technologically advanced society that balanced stability with adaptability. *The Rótbook* chronicled their successes and failures, allowing each path to inform the other over time. This combination of structured innovation and adaptable exploration led the Nyth’rok to become a society that could weather the challenges of the Abyssal Veil, resilient in their deep-rooted philosophy and confident that their choices—like the roots of a great tree—would continue to support them as they reached into the unknown.

## The Impact of The Tide’s Three Consequences

When the Tide swept across the realms, it unleashed forces that defied understanding. This catastrophic phenomenon brought with it *The Three Consequences*, a sequence of disasters that reshaped the world in profound and terrifying ways. The Nyth’rok had no warning, no context, and no expectation for what was to come. To them, the Tide’s arrival was an inexplicable, unfolding nightmare, each consequence bringing new horrors that challenged their survival and threatened their very identity.

### The First Consequence: The Drowning

When *The Drowning* struck, it came without warning. Unlike the Veigrstorm, which the Nyth’rok had sensed as an impending doom—a cosmic disturbance that loomed over them, giving them at least a fleeting moment to brace for its arrival—the onset of *The Drowning* was instantaneous, as swift as it was merciless. There was no time to prepare, no sense of gathering forces. One moment, life in the northern lands carried on as it always had; the next, entire cities were forever altered, warped in ways that defied nature and comprehension.

As if by an unseen hand, entire urban centers became *Stillholds*—places where reality itself fractured, leaving eerie, hollow echoes in the wake of vanished lives. In some *Stillholds*, entire populations simply ceased to exist, while the buildings and streets remained untouched. Homes, temples, and gathering halls stood eerily silent, perfectly preserved yet utterly devoid of life. Ghostly shadows lingered on walls, faint imprints of the people who had once moved through these spaces, as if their presence had been burned into the air itself. The cities became hollow shells, haunted not by spirits but by the absence of those who had once lived there.

In other *Stillholds*, time itself seemed to halt. Within these frozen cities, citizens were locked mid-action, caught in lifelike poses that showed no sign of life or decay. Traders stood frozen in the middle of deals, children paused in play, elders stilled in silent contemplation. An oppressive silence filled these places, a heavy stillness that blanketed every street and home. To those who dared to enter, it felt as if they had stepped into a world abandoned by time, a world that had simply stopped.

For the Nyth’vael, the proud and isolationist core of Nyth’rok society, *The Drowning* was an existential nightmare. Their culture, rooted in stability and careful control, was devastated in an instant. The cities that had once been symbols of security, hoarded resources, and continuity became graveyards of memory. Instead of being fortified by their isolation, they found themselves cut off and vulnerable, their once-thriving society reduced to a fraction of its former self. The Nyth’vael, who had long believed that their philosophy of rootedness would protect them, now faced the horrifying realization that everything they had built could vanish in the blink of an eye.

Their response was marked by disbelief and despair. The Rótbook, their sacred archive of choices and philosophies, still connected them to their past, but it felt hollow in a world where so few of them remained. Rituals felt meaningless without communities to perform them, and hoarded resources were worthless without people to use them. For the Nyth’vael, *The Drowning* was more than a disaster; it was a brutal lesson in the limitations of their isolationist philosophy, a reminder that even the deepest roots could not always hold against forces beyond their control.

### March of the Seedbearers

While the Nyth’vael grappled with the devastation of *The Drowning*, the Nyth’vyr—the *Unrooted*—observed the catastrophe from afar. For some, the eerie silence of the *Stillholds* validated their philosophy, confirming the dangers of isolation. The Nyth’vyr had always believed that resilience lay in adaptability, diversity, and spreading roots across many soils. To them, the plight of the Nyth’vael reinforced the hazards of a rigid, static existence. Though they pitied their kin, these Nyth’vyr chose not to intervene, seeing the tragedy as a consequence of the Nyth’vael’s unwavering philosophy.

But not all Nyth’vyr remained distant. As word of the ghostly *Stillholds* spread, a sense of duty stirred within many of them. This was not a call to simply return home; it was a recognition of their role in saving their people. They knew their homeland was on the brink of collapse, and they felt compelled to help restore what remained of the Nyth’rok. This collective journey became known as *March of the Seedbearers*.

*March of the Seedbearers* was not a pilgrimage of nostalgia or a return to old ways. It was a determined mission to revive the homeland, to breathe life back into the *Stillholds*, and to ensure the survival of the Nyth’rok people. The Nyth’vyr returned from distant lands, bringing with them the resilience and adaptability they had gained through their wanderings. They came not to restore the Nyth’vael’s rigid structures, but to offer fresh perspectives and survival techniques that could stabilize their people. Their goal was to unfreeze the trapped Nyth’rok, to study the eerie imprints in the empty cities, and to find a way to rebuild a society that now teetered on the brink of extinction.

The arrival of the Seedbearers was met with mixed emotions. Some surviving Nyth’vael viewed them with suspicion, seeing the returning Nyth’vyr as outsiders who had turned away from tradition. Yet others saw the Seedbearers as the last hope for survival, understanding that the catastrophe had fundamentally altered their world. The frozen and empty cities were undeniable reminders of the need for adaptability and a willingness to change.

As the Seedbearers moved through the devastated lands, they brought with them skills and knowledge from distant places, blending traditional Nyth’rok wisdom with new ways of thinking. They worked to understand the strange phenomena of the *Stillholds*, hoping to break the stasis that held so many of their kin or, at the very least, bring closure to the surviving communities. They showed the Nyth’vael that *Dýprót*, the Deep Root, could be interpreted not only as rootedness but as resilience—a capacity to grow in unfamiliar soil and adapt to challenges without losing the essence of who they were.

Through the *March of the Seedbearers*, the Nyth’rok began the slow process of rebuilding. The divide between the Nyth’vael and the Nyth’vyr began to soften, as both factions recognized that survival required unity and flexibility. The Nyth’vael, who had once seen the Nyth’vyr’s ways as reckless, now saw the value of adaptability. The Nyth’vyr, who had once dismissed the Nyth’vael’s traditions as stifling, now understood the importance of continuity and cultural memory.

The *Stillholds* remained as solemn witnesses to the first consequence of the Tide, their empty streets and frozen figures haunting reminders of the fragility of existence. Yet, through the *March of the Seedbearers*, the Nyth’rok found a way forward. They emerged from *The Drowning* forever changed, united by the realization that survival required both deep roots and branches that could bend and grow. For the Nyth’rok, the Tide’s first consequence had shown them that the strength of *Dýprót* lay not only in continuity but in the courage to adapt, to rebuild, and to carry their culture forward into an uncertain future.

### The Second Consequence: The Divergence

After *The Drowning* left the Nyth’rok population fractured and their lands filled with haunting *Stillholds*, the second consequence of the Tide brought something equally profound but far less tragic: power. Known as *The Divergence*, this event imbued the Nyth’rok with the ability to manipulate quantum energy, unlocking latent potential in ways that would forever reshape their society. For some, these powers felt like an unwelcome mutation, a transformation beyond their control. For others, *The Divergence* was a gift, a chance to transcend limitations and wield forces that were once thought to belong only to myths and legends.

The powers granted by *The Divergence* branched into several distinct types, each of which allowed for different manipulations of time, space, and possibility. These abilities did not appear uniformly; most Nyth’rok who gained powers only accessed a single “branch,” while a rare few could draw from multiple. The abilities were complex and often dangerous, requiring discipline and focus to master. In time, these branches of power would lead to the formation of new factions, each with its own approach to harnessing quantum energy.

### Quantum Time Manipulation: The Branch of Temporal Control

One of the primary branches of power within *The Divergence* was the manipulation of time itself. This branch, grounded in quantum time manipulation, allowed certain Nyth’rok to bend the flow of time in specific, often limited ways. These powers proved invaluable in battle, granting users the ability to control the very tempo of reality. The key abilities within this branch included:

- **Time Dilation**: The ability to slow down time in a localized area, allowing the user to perceive and move with heightened precision while everything around them appears to crawl. In combat, this power could mean the difference between life and death, giving its user unparalleled reaction speed.

- **Temporal Acceleration**: The opposite of dilation, this ability allowed users to speed up time around themselves, making their actions blur to others. Temporal acceleration enabled its wielders to perform tasks or strikes with near-superhuman quickness, overwhelming opponents in a whirlwind of rapid attacks.

- **Temporal Stasis**: This ability allowed the user to momentarily freeze a target or object in time. Although the duration was brief, it was powerful enough to neutralize threats, hold objects in place, or even preserve something fragile in a suspended state.

- **Quantum Recollection**: A rare and taxing ability, quantum recollection allowed the user to rewind their own immediate past, undoing recent actions or recovering from mistakes. Although limited in scope, it provided a valuable opportunity to correct small errors, albeit with significant mental strain.

Masters of the temporal branch were known for their precision and discipline, as any misuse of their powers could have catastrophic consequences. However, time manipulation was only one aspect of *The Divergence*; others found their abilities rooted in a far more chaotic concept—superposition.

### Quantum Superposition: The Branch of Multiplicity and Possibility

The second major branch of quantum power was based on superposition, the concept that all possibilities exist simultaneously in a web of potential outcomes. Those who wielded this branch could access different versions of themselves, bending space and probability to their advantage. This branch of power was unpredictable, more volatile than time manipulation, but it granted its users abilities that redefined what was possible. Key abilities within this branch included:

- **Quantum Teleportation**: By tapping into the infinite possibilities of where they “could be,” those with this power could shift their position instantly, vanishing from one place and reappearing in another. While limited by distance and concentration, it offered unparalleled mobility and escape in battle.

- **Multiversal Vision**: This ability allowed the user to perceive potential outcomes based on present circumstances, giving them insight into probable futures. In combat or strategy, this foresight was invaluable, allowing its wielder to predict an opponent’s actions or anticipate complex sequences of events.

- **Echo Projection**: By existing in multiple realities briefly, users of this power could create temporary copies of themselves, acting as "echoes" that mirror their movements and attacks. The ability to project these duplicates provided a tactical edge, confusing enemies and amplifying the user’s presence.

- **Probability Shifting**: This subtle power enabled the user to manipulate small factors around them, nudging probability in their favor. Whether altering the trajectory of an arrow or influencing the outcome of an event, probability shifting allowed users to subtly reshape their reality.

Superposition-based powers allowed the Nyth’rok to embrace multiplicity, granting them flexibility in the face of danger. However, alongside time and superposition, *The Divergence* also gave rise to a third, mysterious branch—one that operated on the principle of connection.

### Quantum Entanglement: The Branch of Connection and Influence

The third branch, known as *Quantum Entanglement*, was less understood and more rare, yet deeply potent. Those who wielded entanglement-based abilities could link themselves or others on a fundamental level, creating bonds that transcended distance or even physical form. This branch was often regarded as the most mystical, granting powers that allowed users to connect with and influence others in ways that bordered on spiritual. Key abilities within this branch included:

- **Soulbinding**: This ability enabled the user to link two beings on a quantum level, allowing them to share sensations, thoughts, or even injuries across great distances. Such bonds were difficult to break and often carried a profound sense of connection, creating both camaraderie and vulnerability.

- **Quantum Anchoring**: By anchoring themselves or others to a specific location, users of this ability could create zones where other quantum powers—time, superposition, or entanglement—would struggle to function. These “anchored” areas became sanctuaries, places of stillness immune to temporal or spatial disruption.

- **Empathic Resonance**: A more subtle power that allowed the user to project emotions and mental states onto others through quantum links, influencing moods or inspiring loyalty. This ability was often used to calm allies or intimidate enemies, creating a powerful but delicate form of persuasion.

- **Reversion**: An advanced, risky ability that allowed the user to revert an object, person, or area to a prior state, effectively undoing recent damage or changes. Though it could heal wounds or repair objects, any attempt to “revert” complex systems, like people or environments, carried unpredictable side effects.

Masters of entanglement were known for their introspective nature, often revered as sages or mystics due to their ability to bridge distances both physical and emotional. These three branches of quantum manipulation brought profound changes to the Nyth’rok, leading to the emergence of new factions and ideologies, as well as deeper societal divisions.

### Societal Impact and New Factions

The powers granted by *The Divergence* did more than alter individual Nyth’rok—they fractured and reshaped their society, pushing them into new philosophies, new structures, and ultimately, new factions. While some embraced these changes with curiosity or valor, others took this newfound power as a means to withdraw further into isolation or plunge into the depths of multiversal possibility. In time, these shifts led to the rise of new factions and the solidification of older ones, each dedicated to a unique approach to the manipulation of quantum energy and the purpose of their culture’s survival.

### The Nyth’rundr: Extremists of the Aeternums

Before the Tide’s arrival, a small but fervent faction existed within Nyth’rok society: the *Nyth’rundr*. Named from *“rundr,”* meaning “eternal” or “enduring” in their ancient tongue, the Nyth’rundr were an extreme offshoot of the Nyth’vael, embodying an intense form of isolationism and purity. They believed that the essence of the Nyth’rok could only survive through strict separation from outside influences. Unlike the broader Nyth’vael, who practiced cautious isolationism to preserve tradition, the Nyth’rundr took these ideas to their absolute extreme. They viewed any interaction with outside forces—even certain Nyth’rok factions—as a form of contamination, a threat to the true essence of their people. The Nyth’rundr’s goal was to create a "perfected" Nyth’rok society, untouched by foreign ideas or change.

When *The Divergence* granted the Nyth’rundr the ability to manipulate time, they saw an opportunity to enact their vision of a sealed society. The Nyth’rundr retreated into fortified strongholds known as *Aeternums*, temporal sanctuaries where time flowed according to their will. Surrounded by walls of quantum distortion, each Aeternum was isolated from the natural timeline, allowing the Nyth’rundr to evolve separately from the rest of the world. Some Aeternums sped up time, enabling rapid advancement in knowledge and culture within their cloistered walls. Others slowed to a near-stillness, preserving the Nyth’rundr in states of stasis, like temporal relics, immune to the ravages of the outside world.

To the rest of the Nyth’rok, the Aeternums became places of myth and rumor, cloaked in secrecy. The Nyth’rundr were said to be conducting experiments within these strongholds, seeking ways to sever their society from the outside entirely, achieving a form of timeless existence. The Aeternums were seen as symbols of extreme isolationism, echoing both awe and fear among the Nyth’rok, as few dared approach these enigmatic fortresses. The Nyth’rundr’s retreat marked the farthest extreme of their culture’s desire for preservation, creating a society that would remain untouched, hidden from the world.

### The Nyth’dar: Scholars of the Boundless

Another faction that existed within Nyth’rok society was the *Nyth’dar, derived from “dar,” meaning “insight” or “wisdom*”. Before *The Divergence*, the Nyth’dar were a humble faction of scholars and monks dedicated to preserving and interpreting the Rótbook, acting as custodians of Nyth’rok knowledge and tradition. The Nyth’dar believed that their people’s strength lay in the depth of their history and wisdom, and they took it upon themselves to ensure that every lesson, every choice, was recorded and preserved for future generations. Although they were small in number, their devotion to the Rótbook made them revered figures, as they provided spiritual and intellectual guidance to the larger Nyth’rok community.

After *The Divergence*, the Nyth’dar were transformed by their newfound quantum powers, adopting a new purpose and identity as *The Boundless*. The Boundless saw these powers as a means to explore beyond the limitations of a single reality, using quantum manipulation to unlock the mysteries of time, space, and possibility. To structure their society, they organized themselves into three castes, each one focused on a different temporal realm: past, present, and future. These castes embodied distinct philosophies within the Boundless, each dedicating themselves to a unique mission in service to the Nyth’rok.

1.  **The Tellers of Tomorrow**

    - The *Tellers of Tomorrow* were dedicated to the exploration of potential futures. Using powers like *Multiversal Vision*, they glimpsed possible timelines, studying future outcomes to provide guidance for Nyth’rok society. As seers and prophets, the Tellers of Tomorrow wielded their insights with care, believing that knowledge of the future could shape the present in powerful ways. However, their fixation on probabilities and outcomes often distanced them from the here and now, and many Tellers found themselves lost in a web of potentialities. To the Nyth’rok, they were both revered and feared for their otherworldly insight, as the Tellers of Tomorrow peered into realms of possibility beyond most people’s comprehension.

2.  **The Preservers of the Present**

    - Known as the *Preservers of the Present*, this caste was dedicated to restoring and healing what was lost in *The Drowning*, focusing their efforts on the frozen and empty cities known as *Stillholds*. They believed that the powers granted by *The Divergence* could be used to unfreeze their kin trapped in temporal stasis or bring closure to the empty cities left hollow and lifeless. As the most compassionate and service-oriented of the Boundless, the Preservers of the Present saw themselves as guardians of their people’s immediate reality. They used quantum entanglement and temporal manipulation in attempts to undo the effects of *The Drowning*, viewing the restoration of the *Stillholds* as a sacred duty. The Preservers were deeply respected within the Boundless, as they embodied the mission to reconnect the present to a past that had been cruelly cut off.

3.  **The Pilgrims of the Past**

    - The *Pilgrims of the Past* were dedicated to the preservation and understanding of the Nyth’rok’s ancient roots. Using their powers to access echoes of history and ancestral memories, the Pilgrims delved into the Rótbook’s teachings, reconstructing fragments of lost knowledge to keep the essence of Nyth’rok identity alive. They saw themselves as the memory-keepers of the Boundless, acting as a bridge between the past and the present. By preserving their people’s origins, the Pilgrims of the Past sought to anchor the Nyth’rok amidst the rapid transformations of *The Divergence*. Though often seen as reclusive, the Pilgrims were widely respected as the keepers of tradition, ensuring that the Nyth’rok would not lose themselves in the shifting tides of power and change.

The Boundless, unified by their devotion to temporal exploration and wisdom, became one of the most respected factions within Nyth’rok society. Each caste within the Boundless focused on a different temporal realm, together embodying the philosophy that quantum power was a path to understanding the continuum of existence. The Boundless were revered for their knowledge, though their detachment from the mundane led others to see them as both wise and alien, as if they were constantly gazing beyond the fabric of the ordinary world.

### The Nyth’vyr: Warriors of the March

While the Nyth’rundr and the Boundless withdrew into isolation or contemplative pursuits, the *Nyth’vyr* embraced *The Divergence* as a means to enhance their survival and strength. Known as the *Warriors of the March*, the Nyth’vyr wielded their powers not for study or seclusion, but for combat and resilience. As they continued the *March of the Seedbearers* toward their homeland, they encountered monstrous creatures, hostile enclaves, and the fractured landscapes left by the Tide. Their abilities made them formidable, turning them into legendary figures who could manipulate reality in ways that defied belief.

The Nyth’vyr used superposition and time manipulation to devastating effect in battle. They teleported across the battlefield, created echoes of themselves to disorient enemies, and predicted their opponents’ moves through glimpses of possible futures. With each victory, their reputation grew, and tales of their heroic feats spread across Nyth’rok lands and beyond. They were seen as both defenders and avengers of their people, symbols of the strength and adaptability that the Nyth’rok valued in times of hardship.

Unlike the secluded Nyth’rundr or the introspective Boundless, the Nyth’vyr remained deeply connected to the physical world, dedicating their powers to the protection and restoration of their homeland. They viewed *The Divergence* as a call to action, a sign that their people were meant to survive and reclaim their place in the world. Through their journey and their battles, the Nyth’vyr became a unifying force within Nyth’rok society, embodying the resilience that was needed to face the challenges brought by the Tide.

### The Third Consequence: The Drift

*The Drift* introduced profound disruptions in space and time, creating strange anomalies across Primus. These anomalies caused fragments of other realities and timelines to appear suddenly, displacing entire enclaves from different eras or dimensions and depositing them into the present. This disruption of time further fractured the already unstable world.

For the **Nyth’vael**, these time anomalies were deeply disturbing. The sudden appearance of displaced populations from different timelines represented a destabilizing force that threatened the order they had worked so hard to maintain. They withdrew further into their enclaves, fearing contamination from these displaced societies. Viewing these anomalies as unpredictable and corrupting, the *Nyth’vael* fortified their cities even more, erecting barriers that would protect them from both the physical and metaphysical disturbances of *The Drift*.

The **Nyth’vyr**, however, saw *The Drift* as an opportunity to expand their influence. They ventured into the newly formed enclaves, offering their mercenary services to time-displaced populations and exploring the remnants of other realities for valuable resources. These enclaves were often confused and disoriented by their sudden displacement, and the *Nyth’vyr* became essential to their survival, serving as both guides and protectors in this chaotic new world.

### A Society Transformed

The *Three Consequences* fundamentally transformed the Nyth’rok. The **Nyth’vael**, comprising the majority of the population, retreated into their fortified cities, using their control over time to protect their way of life. They remained deeply isolated, convinced that only through order and discipline could they maintain their superiority in a chaotic world. Meanwhile, the **Nyth’vyr**, though fewer in number, thrived in the disorder, adapting to the new world and using their time manipulation abilities to gain influence as mercenaries. Both factions, though bound by a common heritage, had now fully diverged in their views of survival and control.

## The Nyth’rok Today (Post-Tide)

The *Tide* has come and gone, leaving Primus forever altered by its waves of cosmic and temporal disruption. In the aftermath, the Nyth’rok have emerged as a fractured yet enduring people, defined by their contrasting approaches to survival, power, and their place in the world. Today, the Nyth’rok are split between the dominant **Nyth’vael** and the rare, nomadic **Nyth’vyr**, two factions whose paths diverged long ago during the *Age of the Abyssal Veil* and were further solidified by the events of *The Tide*.

### The Nyth’vael: The Timeless Guardians

The **Nyth’vael** remain the majority faction, controlling a vast network of isolated, fortified enclaves across Primus. These cities are nearly impenetrable to outsiders, meticulously maintained and heavily protected by layers of defense, both physical and temporal. The *Nyth’vael* have perfected their mastery of time manipulation, using it to control every aspect of life within their cities—extending lifespans, enforcing strict social cycles, and ensuring their dominance over the volatile world outside.

Within their enclaves, the *Nyth’vael* live in a state of rigid order, where every moment is carefully measured and accounted for. Time itself has become their ultimate currency, and only those of the highest rank are permitted to wield its full power. Those who show promise in controlling the flow of time rise through the ranks, reinforcing the elite’s hold over their society. Meanwhile, the rest of the population is bound by strict rituals and cycles that ensure the continued stability and endurance of their race.

The *Nyth’vael* view themselves as the rightful rulers of the future. Having survived both the *Undertow* and *The Tide*, they believe that their careful stewardship of time and order will eventually restore the Nyth’rok to their former glory. However, their extreme isolationism has led to growing paranoia. While they hoard their secrets, they fear the chaos of the outside world and see every other race, including the *Nyth’vyr*, as a potential threat to their control. Their cities, hidden behind temporal barriers, are fortresses of both knowledge and fear, and few outsiders have ever seen the heart of a *Nyth’vael* city and lived to tell about it.

### The Nyth’vyr: The Wandering Warriors

In contrast, the **Nyth’vyr** have taken to the open world of Primus, embracing its ever-changing landscapes and unpredictable dangers. They remain rare, scattered across continents as nomadic warriors and elite mercenaries. Their numbers may be few, but their influence far exceeds their population size, as their mastery of time manipulation, combined with their exceptional combat skills, makes them some of the most sought-after fighters and protectors on Primus.

Unlike the *Nyth’vael*, the *Nyth’vyr* see time as a fluid force to be used in the present. They live by the motto that each moment can be shaped and seized, and their reputation as formidable fighters stems from their ability to manipulate time in battle—whether by speeding up their own movements, slowing down their enemies, or bending the flow of time to avoid death itself. This mastery of time has made the *Nyth’vyr* legendary among those who can afford their services, and they have become indispensable allies to many cities, often called upon in times of crisis.

Despite their scattered existence, the *Nyth’vyr* are bound by a shared sense of honor and heritage. They may not cling to the rigid traditions of the *Nyth’vael*, but they take pride in their adaptability, their strength, and their ability to survive in a world that is constantly in flux. Though they rarely stay in one place for long, the *Nyth’vyr* have developed a complex network of allies and clients across Primus, making them a powerful force, even in their nomadic state.

### The Third Consequence: The Drift

The third and final consequence of the Tide was known as *The Drift*. Unlike *The Drowning*, which devastated the Nyth’rok’s homeland, or *The Divergence*, which empowered them with quantum abilities, *The Drift* introduced something altogether stranger and more insidious. Across Primus, enclaves began to appear—fragments of foreign civilizations, cities and communities uprooted from other realms and timelines, deposited seemingly at random across the planet. These enclaves held beings from distant worlds, and perhaps most mysteriously, from a place spoken of as “Earth.” Though unsettling to the inhabitants of Primus, the Nyth’rok, particularly the Nyth’vyr and the Pilgrims of the Past, felt an inexplicable familiarity with these foreign presences.

With *The Drift*, the Nyth’rok found themselves possessing an innate, subconscious understanding of the enclaves’ cultures, languages, and customs. This sense was more than a simple intuition; it was as though they had known these cultures before, despite having no conscious memory of them. The Nyth’rok could not explain this connection, nor did they understand why they could so naturally grasp the traditions and languages of these new inhabitants. They felt an odd sense of kinship with these strangers, an awareness that went beyond mere curiosity. Though they couldn’t identify the source, it was as if some shared history linked them to these enclaves—a bond that lay deep within the quantum echoes awakened by the Divergence.

For the *Nyth’vyr*, especially those on the *March of the Seedbearers*, interactions with these enclaves were infrequent but inevitable. Driven by their mission to return home and assist their kin, they rarely lingered in enclave territories unless directly obstructed. In such cases, the Nyth’vyr’s instinctive understanding of the enclaves allowed them to navigate interactions with remarkable ease, quickly interpreting language and customs to negotiate safe passage. For those Nyth’vyr who had not joined the March, however, this innate familiarity transformed them into intermediaries. These unrooted Nyth’vyr roamed Primus as explorers and emissaries, acting as translators and guides between the enclaves and the established societies on Primus. Though known for their solitary natures, the Nyth’vyr found themselves uniquely suited to bridge the divide between the enclaves and the planet’s native cultures, their quantum-tuned senses revealing pathways of connection that even they couldn’t fully understand.

For the *Pilgrims of the Past*, *The Drift* presented a profound challenge. Traditionally keepers of the Rótbook and guardians of Nyth’rok history, the Pilgrims were rooted in preservation, not exploration. They preferred the stability of their own lands and the quiet sanctity of their records. The devastation of *The Drowning* had only reinforced their cautious approach, leaving them reluctant to venture far from their shelters. Yet, with the arrival of the enclaves, something stirred in the Pilgrims. The enclaves held fragments of history and knowledge that felt oddly, almost painfully familiar—echoes of something they felt they should remember but could not quite grasp. Compelled by this unexplainable connection, the Pilgrims overcame their reservations, setting out beyond their borders to investigate these fragments of forgotten worlds.

As they cautiously engaged with the enclaves, the Pilgrims of the Past began to uncover hints that the history of Primus stretched far deeper and broader than the Rótbook’s accounts. The Rótbook, while respected as one of the oldest records on Primus, held only sparse details from the Age of the Abyssal Veil—a period shadowed in obscurity and lost memories. There were no references to the undertow, no documented origins explaining the existence of Primus’s scattered inhabitants. But as the Pilgrims studied the enclaves, they found clues that suggested the past was more intricate and interconnected than they had realized. Many enclaves spoke of Earth, a name foreign to the Nyth’rok yet strangely resonant, almost like a half-remembered dream. Earth seemed to be a place of origin, a distant world with ties to the enclaves’ histories and perhaps, indirectly, to the Nyth’rok themselves.

Through each interaction, the Pilgrims pieced together fragments from the enclaves—artifacts, symbols, and stories—that hinted at events, civilizations, and cosmic cycles beyond their own recorded past. The stories from Earth and other realms mirrored certain themes in Nyth’rok lore, describing cataclysmic upheavals, cycles of rebirth, and entities that wielded immense, universe-shaping power. Patterns began to emerge, suggesting that Primus and Earth might share a deeper connection, one that had been obscured by time and distance. The Pilgrims couldn’t yet comprehend the full scope of this link, but they recognized that these enclaves offered vital keys to understanding their own origins, perhaps even the forces that had brought about the Tide itself.

The Pilgrims of the Past took on a renewed sense of purpose. No longer merely custodians of the Rótbook, they became seekers of forgotten truths, piecing together a past that extended beyond the known boundaries of Nyth’rok memory. The enclaves gave them a means to explore the roots of Primus itself, leading them to question what had come before the Age of the Abyssal Veil, what had shaped their world, and why these fragments from Earth felt like remnants of a long-lost memory.

For the Nyth’rok as a whole, *The Drift* had transformed their role on Primus. While the Nyth’rundr remained hidden within their Aeternums, sealed off from the world, the Boundless and the Nyth’vyr each embraced the enclaves in their own way. The Boundless, particularly the Tellers of Tomorrow and Pilgrims of the Past, came to view the enclaves as puzzle pieces in a vast, intricate mystery. Their mission grew clearer, becoming part of a larger journey to uncover the true origin and purpose of Primus. In time, these enclaves were no longer seen as mere curiosities or threats—they were echoes of a shared history, fragments of a truth that connected all who dwelled within the Nyth’rok’s ever-deepening understanding of their world.

In the wake of *The Drift*, the Nyth’rok found themselves both explorers and custodians of a fractured reality. Guided by their quantum insights and an instinctive connection to the enclaves, they had begun a journey into the unknown, each encounter drawing them closer to answers about the forces that had shaped Primus and their own place within it. Though the source of their familiarity with the enclaves remained a mystery, the Nyth’rok sensed they were on the edge of rediscovering a long-buried truth, one that might redefine their understanding of existence itself.

## The State of the Nyth’rok Today

In the aftermath of the Tide’s three consequences—*The Drowning*, *The Divergence*, and *The Drift*—the Nyth’rok stand in a period of profound transformation, hovering between survival and renewal. Their society, once united and structured around tradition, is now a patchwork of fractured factions, each adapting in unique ways to the quantum upheaval. The knowledge and powers granted by the Tide have enriched them as much as they have destabilized them, leading the Nyth’rok to question their identity and redefine their place within Primus.

**The Nyth’vael**, once the most powerful and influential faction, have dwindled in both number and reach. Once the heart of Nyth’rok society, they now find themselves isolated within the ghostly *Stillholds*, remnants of their cities frozen in time or emptied of their people. Many Nyth’vael vanished during *The Drowning*, and those who remain cling to what little is left of their fractured communities. The Rótbook still holds their history, and those Nyth’vael who survived remain its staunch guardians. But with each passing generation, their influence wanes, their isolationism and devotion to preservation becoming less relevant in a world now shaped by quantum powers and new alliances. Once the largest and most influential, the Nyth’vael now struggle with a future where they may fade into obscurity, symbols of a once-great order gradually lost to the tides of change.

The **Nyth’rundr** have remained deeply secluded within their Aeternums, their isolation more pronounced than ever. For the Nyth’rundr, *The Divergence* provided the ultimate means of separation, allowing them to control time within their strongholds and sever ties with the outside world. To them, the world beyond the Aeternums has become an unwanted distraction—a place where chaotic forces like the enclaves and the exploration of unknown powers threaten to pollute the purity of their culture. Occasionally, rumors escape from the Aeternums of radical experiments in time manipulation, of entire generations passing within a single day, or of strongholds where time has slowed to a near-standstill. To most Nyth’rok, the Nyth’rundr have become distant legends, symbols of an uncompromising, timeless vision. Yet, in their withdrawal, they may risk becoming relics in a world rapidly evolving beyond their reach.

The **Boundless**, also known by their older name, the ***Nyth’dar***, have risen as one of the most respected factions in Nyth’rok society. They were once a modest group of scholars and record-keepers, stewards of the Rótbook, but *The Divergence* transformed them. Now known as the Boundless, their insights into quantum energy have given them a unique perspective on the new reality shaping Primus. Organized into three castes—*The Tellers of Tomorrow*, *Preservers of the Present*, and *Pilgrims of the Past*—the Boundless each fulfill a specialized role. The Tellers of Tomorrow look ahead, offering counsel on potential futures. The Preservers work to undo the effects of *The Drowning*, focusing their efforts on restoring the *Stillholds* in hopes of rescuing their frozen kin. Meanwhile, the Pilgrims of the Past continue to venture beyond Nyth’rok lands, gathering fragments from the enclaves and piecing together a forgotten history. For the Boundless, the enclaves have deepened their mission, turning them into not just keepers of Nyth’rok culture, but seekers of a grander, unknown legacy that they now suspect may tie them to a wider universe.

**The Nyth’vyr**, with their strength in adaptability and endurance, now play a vital role in the fractured world of Primus. Those who completed the *March of the Seedbearers* returned as hardened, battle-worn figures, carrying stories of their encounters with enclaves, strange creatures, and hostile landscapes. Their presence has brought a new resilience to the Nyth’rok, yet it also challenges the authority of the Nyth’vael, who once held leadership. The Nyth’vyr, especially those who roam freely rather than joining the March, have become reluctant diplomats and emissaries, adapting with ease to the new enclaves and bridging connections where needed. Despite their reputation for being reserved and solitary, their unique ability to intuitively grasp the customs and languages of foreign peoples has turned them into valuable mediators. They stand as a testament to the Nyth’rok’s adaptability, maintaining a bond with the world while representing the unyielding spirit of their people.

Today, the Nyth’rok find themselves at a crossroads. They are a people changed, split between those who cling to tradition and those who seek to understand and integrate the transformations of the Tide. The enclaves continue to bring new knowledge, and the Rótbook grows thicker, no longer a static record but a living testament to the Nyth’rok’s unfolding story. In the face of these changes, the Nyth’rok confront a pressing question: can they reconcile their traditions with the new knowledge shaping their world? Can they remain united in a society where each faction has carved a separate path?

For some, the philosophy of *Dýprót*—the Deep Root—offers guidance. According to Dýprót, each choice the Nyth’rok make is a root that sustains and connects them, providing stability even as their branches reach out toward countless possibilities. To those who hold to this belief, the enclaves and the Divergence are not threats, but branches of a shared origin, pathways that connect them to a wider existence yet to be fully understood. As they move forward, the Nyth’rok find themselves suspended between the past and future, balancing tradition and transformation, and seeking meaning in the unfolding mysteries of Primus. Their fate remains uncertain, yet a quiet determination pulses within each faction—a commitment to honor their roots while daring to reach toward new horizons.

## Anatomy

The anatomy of the Nyth’rok varies significantly across their factions, a reflection of both environmental adaptation and cultural ideals. Though all Nyth’rok share a base physiology—tall, long-limbed, and sturdy—each faction’s appearance embodies its values and lifestyle, often to an extreme.

- **Nyth’vael**: Known for their elegance and reserved demeanor, the Nyth’vael are tall, slender, and hairless, their skin smooth and their features long and refined. Their ears are slightly elongated, lending them an otherworldly look, and their faces often bear a calm, regal expression. Their physique is delicate but strong, built more for resilience and poise than for raw strength. Their eyes, set beneath high brows, are generally pale, appearing almost translucent in certain lights, giving them an aura of quiet authority. The Nyth’vael’s appearance is meticulously maintained, a physical representation of their dedication to order, tradition, and continuity.

- **Nyth’vyr**: The Nyth’vyr, often called the "warriors of the Nyth’rok," are bulkier and more muscular, their powerful builds evoking their Viking ancestry. They often wear their hair long, sometimes braided or tied back, and possess pronounced facial features—strong jaws, sharp cheekbones, and broad noses. Their ears are slightly larger than the Nyth’vael’s, helping them detect subtle sounds in unfamiliar territories. Scars from battle and the elements are common, marking them as seasoned travelers and survivors. They embody strength, adaptability, and a rugged resilience, with eyes that seem perpetually sharp and aware, capturing the essence of their nomadic and independent nature.

- **Nyth’dar**: Resembling the Nyth’vael in structure, the Nyth’dar are also slender and elongated, though they bear a more unkempt appearance. As the scholars and mystics of the Nyth’rok, many are less concerned with personal grooming, allowing their faces to show the wear of years spent in study and exploration. While hairless at youth, some Nyth’dar grow facial hair as they age, giving them a wise, weathered look. Their bodies are generally less toned than other factions, reflecting their dedication to intellectual pursuits over physical ones. The Nyth’dar’s appearance reflects a life of thought and introspection, and their gaze often holds a far-off focus, as if contemplating the mysteries of the multiverse.

- **Nyth’rundr**: The Nyth’rundr are an extreme subset of the Nyth’vael, and their appearance is often the result of genetic manipulation and intense body modification, aiming to reach what they view as the “ideal” form of the Nyth’rok. They are taller than most Nyth’rok, their limbs elongated to unnerving proportions, with features deliberately shaped to highlight symmetry and perfection. Some have unnaturally smooth skin, with altered pigmentation to create a uniform, almost artificial appearance. They rarely display any physical imperfections, their bodies deliberately crafted to reflect their philosophy of purity and perfectionism. This modified appearance, combined with a precise and robotic grace, can make the Nyth’rundr seem more like living statues than organic beings.

## Behavior

Each Nyth’rok faction follows a unique behavioral code rooted in their philosophies and lifestyles, creating a complex society with varied interactions and customs.

- **Nyth’vael**: The Nyth’vael are ritualistic and conservative, adhering to strict codes of behavior and etiquette. They practice daily routines that reinforce their connection to the past and to each other, including gatherings to recite passages from the Rótbook. They value order, loyalty, and continuity, often displaying a reserved demeanor even in the face of hardship. Their interactions are formal, and emotional expression is rare; to the Nyth’vael, controlled and composed behavior is a form of strength. While respectful to others, they can be seen as aloof or dismissive, particularly toward factions they view as disruptive or reckless.

- **Nyth’vyr**: Fiercely independent, the Nyth’vyr are warriors and travelers, driven by adaptability and self-reliance. They rarely travel in groups, and those who embark on the *March of the Seedbearers* live solitary lives, focused on survival and exploration. When two Nyth’vyr meet in the wild, there is a quiet mutual respect, a nod or a gesture acknowledging each other’s strength and independence. Nyth’vyr are blunt and pragmatic, not ones for diplomacy unless necessary. Their behavior often mirrors the rugged landscapes they traverse—stoic, unyielding, and driven by a silent resolve to overcome any challenge that comes their way.

- **Nyth’dar**: The Nyth’dar, as scholars, often appear distant, lost in contemplation or discussion. They are collaborative within their caste, frequently engaging in debates and studies that can last days or even weeks. Driven by intellectual curiosity, they are usually calm, observant, and cautious. The Nyth’dar approach interactions with an inquisitive mindset, valuing knowledge and discovery over conflict. Though generally reclusive, they share their insights willingly when asked, often reciting from memory or theory with the precision and confidence of long-practiced scholars. They show great respect for tradition but are less formal than the Nyth’vael, focused instead on the pursuit of understanding.

- **Nyth’rundr**: The Nyth’rundr are reserved, calculated, and precise in every movement and word. Their behavior reflects an intense dedication to control and perfection, often coming across as cold or detached. They speak only when necessary, and their interactions with others are guided by a strict adherence to their isolationist philosophy. They rarely show emotion, and even among themselves, interactions are limited to discussions of their ideals or the advancements within their Aeternums. They view themselves as the custodians of a perfected Nyth’rok culture, often showing little interest in the affairs of other factions unless it pertains to their ideals of purity.

## Unique Abilities

- **Quantum Perception**: All Nyth’rok possess a heightened sensitivity to quantum space-time, which allows them to intuitively understand the cultures, languages, and customs of the enclaves they encounter. This innate perception enables them to navigate and interact with foreign entities as if guided by ancestral memories, an ability that is strongest among the Boundless (Nyth’dar) and the Nyth’vyr.

- **Time Manipulation**: The Nyth’rundr, with their dedication to perfection, have mastered various forms of time manipulation within their Aeternums. They can alter the flow of time within their strongholds, either speeding it up or slowing it down, making them capable of experiencing years in the span of days or vice versa.

- **Superposition Abilities**: Some Nyth’rok, particularly those of the Nyth’vyr, can use superposition to create temporary clones of themselves, enhancing their abilities in battle or exploration. This skill allows them to appear in multiple places for short periods, a powerful tool for combat and navigation.

## Reproduction

Nyth’rok reproduction is deeply intertwined with their cultural philosophies, with each faction adhering to its own customs and rituals.

- **Nyth’vael**: Reproduction among the Nyth’vael is a structured, almost ceremonial process. Children are seen as continuations of the lineage and culture, so partnerships are chosen with great care. Couples undergo rituals to mark the significance of their union, often reading passages from the Rótbook before conception. Once born, children are raised communally, educated in Nyth’vael values, and taught the history of their people from a young age.

- **Nyth’vyr**: Reproduction among the Nyth’vyr is rare and follows a unique, ritualistic practice. Given their independent nature, Nyth’vyr rarely settle with one another. However, when two Nyth’vyr of the opposite gender meet in the wild, both of age and believing they have sowed their roots deeply enough, they may enter into a brief, intense mating ritual. This ritual is seen as a mutual recognition of their strength and independence. Once children are born, one parent takes on the role of mentor, raising the child as an apprentice while the other continues their journey. The Nyth’vyr place great importance on teaching survival skills, instilling in their offspring the resilience and independence that defines their faction.

- **Nyth’dar**: The Nyth’dar, valuing knowledge over numbers, are selective in reproduction. Many scholars choose to remain childless, viewing their work as their legacy. For those who do choose to have children, reproduction is less ceremonial than among the Nyth’vael. Children are encouraged to seek knowledge from a young age, often raised within scholarly circles, and taught the importance of curiosity, intellectual rigor, and respect for the past.

- **Nyth’rundr**: The Nyth’rundr view reproduction as a means of refinement, often selecting partners based on traits they view as ideal within their philosophy of perfection. Genetic manipulation is common among the Nyth’rundr, with children often subjected to modifications designed to align them with the faction’s standards of physical and mental “purity.” Child-rearing is clinical and regimented, as children are raised to embody the Nyth’rundr’s strict values, with little emphasis on personal identity outside of the faction’s ideals.

## Habitat

The Nyth’rok inhabit various landscapes on Primus, each suited to the needs of their factions:

- **Nyth’vael** primarily live within the *Stillholds* and isolated communities in the north, their habitats echoing their love of tradition and order. Their homes are intricately designed, with clear divisions between living, communal, and ceremonial spaces, allowing them to carry out their structured way of life.

- **Nyth’vyr** roam across diverse terrains, rarely setting down roots. They navigate the wilderness, mountainous regions, and borderlands, preferring places that test their resilience. They set up only temporary camps, designed for efficiency and minimal impact on their surroundings, which allow them to leave quickly when necessary.

- **Nyth’dar** dwell within the archives, libraries, and research sanctuaries scattered across Nyth’rok lands, with many near the Rótbook itself. Their habitats are simple yet filled with texts, artifacts, and resources for study. They favor quiet environments where they can think, read, and experiment in peace.

- **Nyth’rundr** live exclusively within their *Aeternums*, isolated strongholds where time flows at variable rates. These environments are carefully controlled, technologically enhanced, and devoid of any external influences, designed to support their isolationist ideals and controlled experimentation in pursuit of perfection.
