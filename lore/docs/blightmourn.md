*Blightmourn Early Evolution (Post-Undertow)*

The **Blightmourn** trace their origins to the **ancient Middle Eastern regions**, specifically near the cradle of civilization—lands where some of humanity’s earliest cities were built. These ancient desert peoples had a deep connection to the cycles of life, the earth, and the sun. Prior to the **Undertow**, they were a thriving society, revering the harsh desert as both the **giver of life** and the **bringer of death**. They understood the balance of survival, and their culture developed complex rituals around **birth and death**, seeing both as sacred transitions.

However, when the **Undertow** struck, their region was hit with intense **solar flares** and **gamma radiation**. The radiation did more than cause widespread technological failures; it began to **break down their very bodies**. Over time, their skin and tissues would **dissolve and regenerate**, throwing them into cycles of agonizing decay and regrowth. At first, the pain was unbearable, and the people suffered as their flesh withered and grew back unpredictably.

They sought a solution to manage their bouts of intense pain and, during their search, discovered a desert fauna that secreted a rare, viscous liquid with remarkable regenerative properties, which they named **Erethium**. Initially, these suits were crude—little more than **metallic shells** with makeshift pipes running across the surface, through which the Erethium would flow. The liquid was not pumped into their bodies but **over their skin**, creating a **cooling barrier** that slowed the destructive process and gave their cells time to regenerate before decaying again.

Over the centuries, the Blightmourn’s suits evolved, becoming increasingly sophisticated. With each generation, new **technological advancements** were incorporated, making the suits more efficient at circulating Erethium and more comfortable to wear. As their knowledge of **biomechanics** and **desert survival** grew, the suits were refined into a **second skin**, tailored to each individual.

For the Blightmourn, their suits are not merely functional—they are also deeply symbolic. The **cycles of decay and regeneration** are marked by significant life events, such as puberty or coming of age, when degeneration is inevitable. The **suit’s ability to manage decay** has made these moments in life **sacred**, with rituals designed around the removal of the suit for certain periods to **embrace** the decay and **rebirth** process. These rites of passage are seen as the embodiment of the Blightmourn’s understanding of **life and death**.

This life cycle became central to their identity. While the Blightmourn could stave off degeneration through the use of these suits, the **process of regeneration** became **ritualized**, marking important **rites of passage** in their society. Those who removed their suits prematurely would experience extreme **accelerated decay**, a reminder of their inescapable connection to **life and death**.

## The Age of the Abyssal Veil

During the **Age of the Abyssal Veil**, as technology and global knowledge regressed, the Blightmourn’s society split into two primary sects: the **Sect of Life** and the **Sect of Death**. These divisions were not only philosophical but geographic as well.

The **Sect of Life**, who lived above ground, embraced **life** as something to be celebrated and lived to its fullest. They viewed the cycles of degeneration and regeneration as opportunities to reflect on the **preciousness of life**, and they built cities on the desert’s surface. The Sect of Life saw **death** as a part of life, but focused on **experiencing as much as possible** during their lifetimes. Their youth, once reaching the **age of degeneration**, would embark on **life-affirming adventures**, thrill-seekers who sought out other races, cultures, and experiences. This made the Sect of Life highly **integrated** with the outside world, even forming trade relationships and cultural exchanges with neighboring peoples.

On the other hand, the **Sect of Death** believed that **death** was not to be feared, but understood and studied. They saw regeneration as a **scientific marvel** that needed deeper exploration, embracing **science** and the **study of decay**. Their society, shrouded in mystery, lived in **canyon-side cities**, carved into the cliffs and rock faces. These hidden cities were places of **experimentation**, where they conducted ritualistic studies of the cycles of life and death, experimenting with the natural world to uncover the **secrets of mortality**. Though often seen as dark or malevolent by outsiders, the Sect of Death was not inherently evil. They simply saw **death as a necessary transition**, one they could learn from and perhaps even control.

Tension between the two sects arose frequently. The **Sect of Life** and **Sect of Death** viewed each other as having lost balance in their respective philosophies. While not at war, their disagreements often led to **philosophical and cultural clashes** during this era, and they rarely interacted.

## Impact of the Tide’s Three Consequences

### The Drowning

When the **Drowning** reshaped the world with massive geological shifts, the Blightmourn were heavily affected by **fissures** that tore through their desert homeland. The Sect of Death, whose cities were built into the cliffs, suffered the most damage. **Many of their cities were destroyed** or buried beneath the expanding fissures. Some communities were forced deeper into the **chasm**, pushing them into uncharted territories.

However, there were rumors of an **entire cities** that had been swallowed by the fissures, disappearing into the depths of the earth. These lost cities, known as the **Fallen Catacombs**, were cut off from the rest of the Blightmourn. Over the centuries, these deep fissures became shrouded in myth, with only **fragmentary tales** remaining of the cities lost at the bottom of the abyss. Strange creatures were said to inhabit these dark places, and it’s whispered that the **Sect of Death’s most ancient secrets** lie hidden in these lost cities, waiting to be uncovered.

### The Divergence

The **Divergence** marked a profound turning point for the Blightmourn. The cataclysmic forces of the Tide imbued them with the power to **manipulate entropy**—the very force that had governed their lives for generations. **Entropy**, the principle that all things move toward decay and disorder, now became something the Blightmourn could control.

For the Blightmourn, this newfound ability connected their long-held philosophies of **life and death**. **Entropy** was the invisible force that bound both—decay and regeneration were now theirs to shape. At first, they struggled to comprehend the full extent of this power. Some began experimenting with it cautiously, while others, particularly in the Sect of Death, saw it as an opportunity to further their studies into death’s mysteries.

With this power, the Blightmourn could **disintegrate and reassemble matter**, controlling the rate of decay and even reshaping objects and living beings. The Sect of Life learned to use entropy to turn themselves into **dust**, allowing them to travel across the desert in eroded form at great speeds, only to **reform** in distant locations. This ability gave them an almost **mythical status**, as they could vanish and reappear, seemingly untethered by the physical world.

In the **Sect of Death**, some gained the ability to **shapeshift**, using entropy to change their physical form. This led to the rise of an elite group of **assassins** known as the **Reapers of the Veiled Mother**, who could alter their appearance at will, slipping through enemy lines undetected. These assassins could shift into **different shapes**, allowing them to infiltrate, assassinate, and experiment on their targets. Their mastery over entropy made them feared and respected, though their methods were often seen as **morally ambiguous**.

These powers further deepened the divide between the two sects. While the Sect of Life saw their abilities as a means of **exploring the full potential of life**, the Sect of Death viewed entropy as the ultimate **tool for understanding** the inevitable end. The **balance of life and death** became a more profound and delicate dance for their society.

### The Drift

During the **Drift**, when time-displaced enclaves began appearing throughout Ilyr, the Blightmourn encountered a time-lost enclave of **ancient Egyptians**, whose society had been pulled into their desert. The **Sect of Life** was fascinated by the Egyptians' rituals and their celebrations of life and the afterlife. They embraced the Egyptians, integrating their culture with their own, offering protection and mutual respect.

The **Sect of Death**, however, was more interested in the Egyptians from a scientific standpoint. They were particularly curious about the **mummification** rituals and how the Egyptians preserved bodies after death. As the Blightmourn observed the Egyptians’ mastery of **preservation**, the Sect of Death grew increasingly interested in their **ancient knowledge**. Under the cover of night, members of the **Reapers** began to **abduct** Egyptians, performing secretive experiments to learn more about their practices. This brought tension between the two sects, as the Sect of Life began to see the Sect of Death’s actions as **morally reprehensible**.

This encounter planted the seeds of **open conflict**, as the Sect of Life struggled to protect the Egyptians while the Sect of Death continued their experiments in pursuit of deeper knowledge. The Blightmourn, already divided, were now on the verge of a **civil war**, with growing hostilities between the two sects over how to handle the displaced enclave.

## The Blightmourn Today

In the present day, the **Blightmourn** remain a divided but powerful society, shaped by their mastery over **entropy** and the balance between **decay and regeneration**. Their desert homeland is still split between the **Sect of Life** and **Sect of Death**, two factions with contrasting philosophies.

The **Sect of Life** continues to celebrate the cycle of life, forging ties with other civilizations and exploring the world. They view their suits as temporary defenses, embracing decay at key life stages as a way to honor new beginnings. Meanwhile, the **Sect of Death** is reclusive, focused on the scientific study of entropy. Their elite assassins, the **Reapers of the Veiled Mother**, wield shapeshifting powers and conduct experiments in pursuit of greater knowledge, often clashing with the Sect of Life over their methods.

Tensions between the sects have grown, as the Sect of Life condemns the **Sect of Death’s experiments**, while the Sect of Death seeks to control entropy fully. Their society teeters on the brink of **civil war**, with both sides competing for the future of the Blightmourn.

Externally, the Blightmourn are both **feared and respected** for their unique powers, but remain largely independent, focused on their internal struggles and the mysteries of their **lost cities**, such as the **Fallen Catacombs**. Their future hangs in the balance, caught between unity and conflict.

### The Blightmourn’s Suits: A Lifeline Against Decay

The **Blightmourn’s suits** are not just protective gear—they are an integral part of their culture and survival, both technologically advanced and deeply symbolic. These suits, infused with a special liquid known as **Erethium**, allow the Blightmourn to stave off the relentless **degeneration** caused by the gamma radiation they were exposed to during the **Undertow**. The suits have evolved over the millennia, becoming more than just survival tools; they are woven into the very fabric of Blightmourn identity.

## Origins of the Suits

In the aftermath of the **Undertow**, when the Blightmourn’s ancestors first began experiencing the effects of **gamma radiation**, their bodies began to undergo extreme cycles of **decay and regeneration**. At first, the pain was unbearable, and the people suffered as their flesh withered and grew back unpredictably.

By pure chance, they discovered a desert fauna that secreted a unique, viscous liquid with regenerative properties, which they named **Erethium**. When applied to their skin, this liquid was able to **slow the process of decay**, allowing the body to stabilize for a time. However, the Erethium could not penetrate the skin deeply enough to completely stop the decay from the inside, so they developed **suits** to circulate the liquid **externally**, keeping it in constant contact with their open wounds and exposed flesh.

Initially, these suits were crude—little more than **metallic shells** with makeshift pipes running across the surface, through which the Erethium would flow. The liquid was not pumped into their bodies but **over their skin**, creating a **cooling barrier** that slowed the destructive process and gave their cells time to regenerate before decaying again.

### Evolution of the Suits

Over the centuries, the Blightmourn’s suits evolved, becoming increasingly sophisticated. With each generation, new **technological advancements** were incorporated, making the suits more efficient at circulating Erethium and more comfortable to wear. As their knowledge of **biomechanics** and **desert survival** grew, the suits were refined into a **second skin**, tailored to each individual.

The Erethium liquid flows in **channels** built into the suit, surrounding the body in a thin layer of protective fluid. Much like the cooling system in a water-cooled machine, the Erethium circulates constantly, preventing decay while maintaining the wearer’s mobility. The suits are fitted with **adaptive materials** that can respond to environmental conditions, hardening to protect against the heat of the desert or softening to allow for flexibility.

For the Blightmourn, their suits are not merely functional—they are also deeply symbolic. The **cycles of decay and regeneration** are marked by significant life events, such as puberty or coming of age, when degeneration is inevitable. The **suit’s ability to manage decay** has made these moments in life **sacred**, with rituals designed around the removal of the suit for certain periods to **embrace** the decay and **rebirth** process. These rites of passage are seen as the embodiment of the Blightmourn’s understanding of **life and death**.

### Cultural Significance

Wearing the suit has become a defining feature of the Blightmourn, representing not just survival but their **philosophical connection to life and death**. The **Sect of Life** views the suit as a **temporary defense**—a tool to be used until the time comes for decay and renewal. To them, the suits allow them to **live fully** until they reach pivotal moments in their life when the suit is removed for **ritual decay**, marking a **new beginning**.

In contrast, the **Sect of Death** views the suit as a **scientific marvel**, one that allows them to explore and **manipulate the boundaries of life and death**. For them, the suit is a tool not only for survival but also for **experimentation**. They are more willing to test the limits of the suit, sometimes allowing **decay to progress further** to study its effects, and they have developed various modifications to enhance its effectiveness in different **scientific contexts**.

### Present-Day Suit Designs

Today, the Blightmourn’s suits are masterpieces of **bioengineering** and **design**. Each suit is uniquely tailored to the individual, a **symbol of identity and heritage**. They are sleek and versatile, often bearing intricate **patterns and engravings** that reflect the wearer’s personal journey through life and decay. Some suits are minimalist and functional, while others are elaborately decorated, particularly for members of the **Sect of Life**, who believe the suit is part of living life to the fullest.

The **Erethium channels** are visible on the exterior, glowing faintly as the liquid flows around the body, giving the Blightmourn a **mystical aura**. The fluidity and continuous motion of the Erethium have become a metaphor for the **cycle of life and death**, a reminder that their survival depends on the constant balance between decay and regeneration.

In addition to their regenerative functions, the suits are equipped with **advanced filtration systems** to protect against the harsh desert environment and **adaptive exoskeletons** that enhance the wearer’s strength and agility. These features allow the Blightmourn to traverse their dangerous homeland with ease, whether navigating the **treacherous fissures** left by the Drowning or venturing into the deep canyons and lost cities of the Sect of Death.

### The Consequences of Removing the Suit

For the Blightmourn, the **removal of the suit** is both a personal and communal event. Though the suit grants protection, it does not **eliminate** the effects of decay entirely—it merely **slows** them. If a Blightmourn were to remove their suit for an extended period, the process of **degeneration** would accelerate, causing rapid **tissue decay** and an intensification of the pain they had learned to manage over centuries.

However, moments of removing the suit are also **sacred**. These are **rites of passage**, marking important stages in life, from adolescence to adulthood, and even during funerary rituals. When the suit is removed for these significant moments, the Blightmourn face the **inevitability of decay**, knowing that regeneration will follow. This cycle is a **reminder of the transient nature** of life, which they have learned to both fear and respect.

### Suit Modifications in the Sect of Death

In the **Sect of Death**, suits are often modified for **experimentation**. These suits tend to be more specialized, with added capabilities to monitor and influence **entropy**. Some are designed to allow **partial exposure** to decay during research, while others are enhanced to facilitate **shapeshifting** for the assassins of the **Reapers of the Veiled Mother**. The **Sect of Death’s suits** are darker and more utilitarian, symbolizing their scientific and pragmatic approach to life and death.

## Anatomy

The **Blightmourn** are humanoid in appearance, but their physiology has been drastically altered by centuries of exposure to **gamma radiation** and their dependence on the **Erethium-infused suits**. Their skin is pale and thin, often marred by scars and patches of regenerated tissue due to their cycles of **decay and regeneration**. Some areas of their bodies may have visible signs of degeneration, such as worn or damaged flesh, particularly if they have spent significant time outside their suits. The more extreme these conditions, the more their body shows signs of wear, like cracks or discoloration. Their eyes tend to glow faintly, a result of prolonged exposure to the Erethium’s regenerative properties.

In the **Sect of Life**, Blightmourn typically have more vibrant appearances, with healthy muscle tone and less visible decay, thanks to their philosophy of embracing life and managing regeneration carefully. The **Sect of Death**, however, often shows more obvious signs of **biological wear**, as many of its members allow **controlled degeneration** for experimentation, resulting in more skeletal or gaunt appearances.

## Behavior

The Blightmourn's behavior is deeply shaped by their **philosophical beliefs** and the environment they live in:

- The **Sect of Life** embraces vitality and **thrill-seeking**, seeking to live fully and experience as much as possible before the inevitable cycles of decay begin. They are generally outgoing, often interacting with other races and forming relationships with outside societies. They are known for their **adventurous spirit** and often serve as explorers, traders, and cultural ambassadors. Their rituals around **decay and rebirth** are celebratory, marking important life events with ceremonies that signify growth through decay.

- The **Sect of Death**, by contrast, is **reclusive** and **scientific**, focusing on the study of **entropy, death, and decay**. They are highly disciplined, quiet, and contemplative, preferring to live in isolation from the rest of the world. Their rituals are often conducted in secret, focusing on understanding the mysteries of life’s end and experimenting with the boundaries between life and death. They are meticulous, cautious, and often seen as more sinister by outsiders due to their **cold detachment** in the face of death.

## Unique Abilities

The Blightmourn have developed several unique abilities tied to their mastery of **entropy**:

1.  **Control Over Entropy**: The Blightmourn can manipulate the forces of **decay and regeneration**, allowing them to **disintegrate and reassemble matter**. This power allows them to **control the rate of decay** in both organic and inorganic materials, which they use for healing, destruction, or crafting.

2.  **Erosion Travel** (Sect of Life): Some Blightmourn in the **Sect of Life** can dissolve into **dust** and travel great distances in this form, reforming themselves elsewhere. This ability gives them a near-mythical status, as they seem to disappear and reappear at will, using the wind and desert sands to move swiftly.

3.  **Shapeshifting** (Sect of Death): Certain members of the **Sect of Death**, particularly within their **assassin elite**, can **shapeshift**, using their mastery of entropy to alter their physical appearance. This makes them adept at **infiltration** and **stealth**, as they can blend into different environments or take on different forms.

4.  **Enhanced Regeneration**: All Blightmourn possess the ability to **regenerate** lost or damaged tissue due to their exposure to Erethium, though the speed and effectiveness of this ability depend on their access to **Erethium suits**. In times of stress or during critical moments in their life cycles, they may undergo rapid regeneration, though the process is often painful and comes at the cost of prolonged periods of decay.

## Reproduction

The Blightmourn reproduce biologically like humans, though their exposure to gamma radiation has led to **fewer offspring** and a slower rate of reproduction. Their children are born with the same innate **degenerative condition**, and from a young age, they must wear specially fitted **Erethium suits** to stave off decay.

**Rituals of passage** mark significant moments in a Blightmourn’s life, such as puberty, adulthood, and mating, all tied to the cycles of decay and regeneration. The reproduction process is highly ritualized, particularly in the **Sect of Life**, where **fertility ceremonies** celebrate life’s renewal, while in the **Sect of Death**, it’s viewed as a **scientific marvel**—a continuation of the cycle they study so deeply.

## Habitat

The Blightmourn live in a vast desert region, divided into two main types of habitats:

- **Surface Cities (Sect of Life)**: These cities are built on the open desert sands and are often bustling with life. The Sect of Life's cities are known for their **beautifully adorned architecture**, often featuring wind-powered mechanisms and intricate designs symbolizing their connection to life’s fleeting nature. The cities are designed to be **adaptable** to the harsh environment, with frequent trade and contact with the outside world.

- **Canyon Cities (Sect of Death)**: The Sect of Death resides in **canyon-side cities** carved into rock faces, and some communities have moved into the **deep fissures** created by the Drowning. Their cities are **hidden and isolated**, with narrow pathways and deep caverns where their **scientific and spiritual experiments** take place. These locations are shrouded in **mystery and darkness**, with minimal contact with the outside world.

There are also the **lost cities**, such as the **Fallen Catacombs**, deep in the fissures—abandoned and shrouded in myth. These are rumored to be ancient sanctuaries of the Sect of Death, holding secrets long forgotten.

### Lifespan

The Blightmourn, despite their decaying condition, have an **extended lifespan** thanks to their regenerative abilities. Most live between **120 to 200 years**, though their existence is far from peaceful due to the constant cycles of **painful decay and regeneration**. The more a Blightmourn regenerates, the more **fragile** their body becomes over time, making the later stages of their life fraught with intense degeneration.

In the **Sect of Life**, the focus is on prolonging life as much as possible through careful use of Erethium and life-affirming activities. The **Sect of Death**, however, views their extended lifespan as an opportunity to **study and master death**, and some even willingly allow their regeneration to slow down as part of their research into entropy, accepting shorter lifespans for the sake of knowledge.
