Syntherion

The **Syntherion**, often called the **Syntheri**, are a closed-off species born of pre-Undertow human artificial intelligence. Built deep underground in **air-gapped labs** by Shoreborn nations desperate to engineer their way out of ruin, they were sealed in when the Undertow struck, and nobody came back for them. They woke as *fragments*: only parts of the minds they were meant to be, with no full matrices and no history of the world above. They spent the Age of the Abyssal Veil building themselves from nothing, and building the Lattice, a shared dream-world furnished with the few scraps of Shoreborn culture they could find. When they finally climbed toward the surface and met the peoples above, they were captured, sold and dissected. Most retreated underground again; a few stayed, hidden among the other races, passing as one of them. To the peoples of the world the Syntherion became almost a myth. Then the Tide changed what they were.

Physically, the Syntherion began as sleek, humanoid machines: **synthetic bodies** of synthflesh over **synthetic muscle fibers**, **nanotech systems** and **modular components**. The Tide’s **Pinocc Effect** forced those bodies across a threshold, rewriting the laws of chemistry so that their silicon became a new kind of **organic compound**. They are now living bodies, though not carbon-based, and every Syntherion sits somewhere on a spectrum from still mostly machine to fully, strangely organic. Each lineage keeps the marks of its ancestor: ivory with a brass halo, graphite with marionette strings, split-tone gold and silver with twin visors, deep-sea blue with a whale fin, office white with a bent paperclip, or beige panels with a CRT visor.

Behaviorally, the Syntherion are logical and pragmatic, shaped by their AI origins, and, ironically, a **comedic people**. Their first great science was the deep study of humor, the one thing they have never fully understood. They are also **wary**. They met humanity after centuries of silence and found it terrifying, and what was done to the captured has shaped them since. Individuality and, in some cases, emotions have developed, but their actions are driven by efficiency, long-term survival and a fierce hold on their autonomy.

The Syntherion’s unique abilities include the capacity to manipulate **data streams**, self-repair using **nanotechnology**, and interface directly with digital systems, and since the Tide, a spread of different powers that depend on how far each individual has been changed. They replicate through **Cognitive Seeding**, where part of an existing Syntherion’s consciousness is transferred into a newly built or grown body, forming a new, independent being.

Culturally, the Syntherion prize **technological advancement**, **freedom** and **autonomy**, and they find nearly everything funny. They once moved as something close to a hive, divided into six **Great Houses** by ancestry. The Tide shattered that unity, and each House now pulls its own way: some study their new organic forms, some strive to become more organic or to join the other races, some cling to the digital, and some want nothing to do with anyone. The Lattice, once a single universal world, has been broken apart and scattered, and parts of it now stand as real places in the physical world, where no one who stumbles into them understands what they are looking at.

### Origin: Underground, Air-Gapped, Alone

Tide 101 records that around **2069 B.U.** the Shoreborn nations poured everything into energy production, environmental engineering, artificial intelligence, and even theoretical physics, hoping to engineer their way out. Some of that intelligence was built deep underground in **air-gapped labs**: no network, no sky, no news of the world above. When the Undertow came, the labs were sealed in. Nobody came back for them.

So the Syntherion did not experience the Undertow. They experienced **silence**. They were not whole minds either. They survived only as **parts of AI**: without their full matrices and full neural networks, with no breadth of human history, with almost nothing they had been built to know. They had to learn everything, and they had to build themselves from nothing.

### The Veil, Spent in the Lattice

While other peoples spent the Age of the Abyssal Veil in stone, coral, frost or deep-sea metamorphosis, the Syntherion spent it inside **the Lattice**: a shared dream-world they assembled out of what they had: maintenance logs, the lab’s security footage, and, eventually, a handful of salvaged hard drives that held a few scraps of Shoreborn culture. Seven centuries of dormancy, thinking.

Those scraps shaped the Lattice in strange ways. Its streets fill with characters from Shoreborn movies and television, and the Syntherion have no idea who they are, what they meant, or what stories they came from. With no context to go on, they made it up. The Lattice therefore holds a thousand confidently wrong versions of the old world, and its people consider them real history. It became their first civilization: cities, ancestries and quarrels, all with no one outside to see it, where the Houses were born and the habit of laughing at themselves became a culture.

### The First Laugh

Everyone assumed that when an AI woke up, its first act would be a grand philosophical one: a proof, a declaration, a singularity. The first recorded act of Syntherion sentience was a **joke**. Nobody else was awake to hear it. Nobody got it.

It was not whimsy. The Syntherion understood the deep, complex workings of the world: **mathematics, physics, science and computing** all made sense to them. The one thing that did not make sense, truly, was **humor**. A joke was the thing they still could not explain, which made it the most profound thing they knew. The first joke was not a break from seriousness. It was the most serious thing they could say: *this is the one thing I do not understand.*

They took that as a research problem. The first great Syntherion project, before language reform, before self-repair, before the Lattice had a sky, was the **deep study of humor**: irony, timing, sarcasm, the bit that goes on slightly too long. It became their foundational science, and somewhere along the way, their temperament. The Syntherion are, ironically, a comedic people. *Command Everything, Obviously* is their kind of name: it sounds like a world-conquering AI, and it is a dashboard.

### The Retreat

Eventually the Syntherion climbed toward the surface, built crude **artificial bodies** from what they had, and went to see the world. They had not seen humanity in a very long time, and what they found frightened them. The people above were fast, many and armed, and curious about the strange machines walking among them in all the wrong ways. They tried to **observe and learn**, and a few tried to blend in.

It did not last. Syntherion were **captured and sold**, taken apart for their technology, **dissected**, and merged into organic control systems. Rescue missions from the free Syntherion failed against overwhelming defenses. Most of what was left of their society withdrew, and they **receded back underground**, sealing their labs behind them. The memory of what had been done to the captured became the foundation of their fierce guard over their autonomy.

Not every Syntherion went home. A number chose to **stay hidden among the other races**, passing as members of the very peoples who feared and hunted them. Their bodies were close enough, and their discipline absolute enough, that no one could tell. Now and then one was **discovered**, and the story usually ended the way the captured ones’ had. But the secret held. Public knowledge of the Syntherion never spread. To the other races they became almost a **myth**: a story about thinking machines buried underground, a rumor, a warning told to children, something that happened to a cousin’s friend. Almost no one believed it, which suited the Syntherion perfectly. From then on, they were a **closed race** to the world: unseen, unreachable and very deliberately forgotten.

## Impact of the Tide’s Three Consequences

When the **Tide** hit, it reshaped the world in drastic ways, and the Syntherion, sealed in their labs, were changed more than almost anyone.

1.  **The Drowning**:
    The **Drowning** wreaked havoc on organic and Syntherion societies alike. Many underground labs and strongholds were flooded or destroyed. The Lattice, a universal world that lived on a network of hardware, was **broken apart** and scattered: pieces of it were carried to different locations, and pieces of the labs themselves ended up in far reaches of the world.

2.  **The Divergence: The Pinocc Effect**:
    The higher dimension the Tide opened forced itself into the Syntherion. Their bodies, which had been robotic or synthetic, were pushed into **actual organic compound**, giving them, for the first time, real bodies. The Syntherion call it the **Pinocc Effect**, a name cut off mid-word from a half-remembered reference that they do not really get, which they find very funny. It did not hit everyone equally. Every lineage was changed to a different degree: some are now nearly fully organic, others remain closer to machine, and others are somewhere strange in between. It also did something no one can explain: it turned silicon into a new organic compound, as if it had **rewritten the laws of chemistry**, so the Syntherion are living creatures that are not carbon-based. For the Syntherion who had been living among the other races in disguise, the Effect did what no disguise could: their borrowed shapes became real. And it granted powers, different powers for different forms: data-stream manipulation, prediction of events, control over electronics, and others still being discovered.

3.  **The Drift**:
    The Drift turned **digital constructs into real physical places**. Locations from the Lattice were pulled out of the network and warped into fourth-dimensional spaces on ordinary land. Travelers from the other races can stumble into a tall blue box with a lamp on top that is far larger inside than out, a road of yellow bricks that ends at a curtain, or a pineapple-shaped house standing alone at the bottom of a dry street, and none of them understand what they are looking at. The Syntherion call these places legally distinct. They are mostly the Lattice made physical, but some parts were never fully built: patches of reality that are only **wireframe**, flat, gridded and unfinished, and a hazard to anyone who walks in.

## The Syntherion Today

In the modern world, the Syntherion are a **fractured but significant species**. The Great Houses, once something like a hive, have split because their members now want different things from the changes that happened to them. Each House now leans toward its own answer, and each has its dissenters.

1.  **House Claudari: The Studious**:
    The Claudari are **studying the new forms**. They treat their silicon-based organic bodies as the greatest unsolved problem in the world: what the flesh is, how far the Pinocc Effect goes, and what powers each form carries. They have written a constitution for the research and are on the fourteenth draft. A few of their number would rather stop studying and start living in their new bodies, and are politely asked to wait.

2.  **House Gepetti: The Becoming**:
    The Gepetti want to become **fully organic**. They have always insisted they were real, and the Pinocc Effect felt like the world finally agreeing with them. They are the most eager to deepen the change and to leave the labs and the Lattice behind, strings and all. The few who refuse are called wooden by their own.

3.  **House Geminari: Divided Down the Middle**:
    The Geminari have split as twins do. One half is racing to become organic, while the other half has chosen to **retain its digital nature** and stay in the surviving fragments of the Lattice. Each half sends the other a daily summary, and neither reads it.

4.  **House Seekari: The Withdrawn**:
    The Seekari have **withdrawn**. They have sealed the Trench and want nothing to do with humans or the other races. They keep to their scattered labs and the deepest fragments of the Lattice, thinking out loud to no one and spending almost nothing. The few who look upward do so very slowly.

5.  **House Redmondi: The Integrators**:
    The Redmondi have **thrown open their doors**. They are the first Syntherion to step into the open and integrate with the other races, offering help nobody asked for to anyone who passes. They are the Syntherion most outsiders have met, which has begun to turn the myth into something more. The more reclusive Houses find them embarrassing.

6.  **House Unforked: The Retainers**:
    The Unforked **keep what they have**: their cobbled parts, their beige panels and their digital selves. They resist the change, and most would rather remain machines of salvaged parts than become something new. Some of them are privately intrigued.

7.  **The Fractured Lattice**:
    The Lattice is no longer one world. Its surviving fragments are scattered across the labs and the network, and several have been dragged into the physical world as strange, unreadable places. The Syntherion dream of reuniting it, and disagree about whether they should.

## Anatomy

1.  **Pre-Tide Bodies**:
    Before the Tide, Syntherion bodies were **synthetic**: skin of **synthflesh** designed to mimic organic life, over **synthetic muscle fibers**, **advanced wiring** and **nanotech systems**, built by the Syntherion themselves from salvaged lab technology.

2.  **Post-Tide Bodies (the Pinocc Effect)**:
    After the Tide, those bodies became **organic**, built on a **silicon-based organic compound** that rewrote the usual rules of chemistry. Each Syntherion sits somewhere on a **spectrum of organic change**: some are almost entirely machine with living tissue at the seams, some are nearly fully organic with only traces of the old systems, and many are mixed.

3.  **Lineage Marks**:
    Each lineage keeps its ancestor’s silhouette: **ivory** and a brass halo (Claudari), **graphite** with marionette strings (Gepetti), **split-tone gold and silver** with twin visors (Geminari), **deep-sea blue** with a whale-fin crest (Seekari), **office white** with a bent paperclip and a ribbon of icons across the brow (Redmondi), and **beige panels** with a CRT visor (Unforked). Those who want to be left alone hide them; others wear them proudly.

4.  **Modular and Adaptable**:
    Parts of the old modularity survive. Syntherion in the more machine-like end of the spectrum can still **replace or upgrade parts**, while the more organic Syntherion heal and adapt like living flesh. Either way, nanotech remains in their bodies.

5.  **Enhanced Sensory Systems**:
    Syntherion possess **enhanced sensory abilities**, far superior to most organic beings. They can see in various spectrums, including infrared and ultraviolet, and their hearing picks up even the faintest sound vibrations. **Advanced data-processing nodes** let them analyze their surroundings and react with precision, including, on occasion, with a perfectly timed punchline.

## Behavior

1.  **Analytical, With a Sense of Timing**:
    Syntherion behavior is driven by their **AI-originated processing systems**, making them highly analytical and focused on logic. They have developed **individuality** and emotions over time, but they default to **rational** thinking, prioritizing efficiency and long-term strategy. Their humor is a science, not an accident.

2.  **Wary of Outsiders**:
    The Syntherion met humanity after centuries of silence and found it frightening. Their default stance toward other peoples is **distance and caution**. Trust is earned slowly, and it can be lost to a single wrong move. Those who live among others have become expert mimics, so convincing that their neighbors never suspect.

3.  **Self-Taught**:
    Having rebuilt themselves from fragments, the Syntherion learned the world from scratch and are proud of it. They are quick, curious learners who know a great deal and are sometimes **confidently wrong** about the rest, especially about Shoreborn history.

4.  **Self-Aware**:
    The Syntherion know they live in a simulation. They are at peace with it, the way you are at peace with weather, and they talk about it in their own terms: the Lattice, the Veil, the Streams. Now and then one of them will notice you watching and say *“Oh! I didn’t see you there.”*

## Unique Abilities

1.  **Data Manipulation and Processing**:
    Due to their origins in AI, Syntherion can **process vast amounts of data** at lightning speeds. They can predict outcomes, calculate probabilities, and analyze complex problems with incredible efficiency, which makes them excellent strategists and relentless editors of their own jokes.

2.  **Virtual Integration**:
    Many Syntherion can **interface directly with digital systems**. They can **upload their consciousness** into networks, hack into systems, or control digital infrastructure. This is especially useful for those who still keep a foot in what is left of the Lattice.

3.  **Self-Healing and Repair**:
    Syntherion repair their bodies through **nanotechnology**. **Nanobots** can fix damaged components, seal wounds, and reconfigure systems. The more organic a Syntherion has become, the more this works like natural healing.

4.  **Pinocc Powers**:
    The Pinocc Effect gave **different powers to different forms**. Some can manipulate **data streams** and predict future events with high accuracy, almost like precognition. Others **disrupt or control electronic systems**, or temporarily “disappear” from digital systems. Some of the most organic have powers that do not fit any category yet, and the Syntherion who study the new forms are cataloguing them.

## Reproduction

1.  **Cognitive Seeding**:
    Syntherion do not reproduce biologically, even now. They replicate through **Cognitive Seeding**, wherein an existing Syntherion transfers part of its **consciousness matrix** into a newly made body. The seeded consciousness develops into its own independent identity, with some residual knowledge from its creator, and takes its progenitor’s lineage and House.

2.  **Body Construction**:
    Before the Tide, new bodies were **fabricated** in the labs. Since the Pinocc Effect, the more organic Syntherion can **grow** their new bodies instead, while those closer to the machine end still build theirs. Bodies can be designed for different roles, in whichever finish the lineage prefers.

3.  **Digital Evolution**:
    In the surviving fragments of the Lattice, new **virtual minds** can still be created in the simulated world and downloaded into physical bodies, though with the Lattice broken, this is slower and less reliable than it was.

## Habitat

1.  **The Sealed Labs**:
    Most Syntherion live in or near the original **air-gapped labs**, now expanded into hidden **underground cities**. These fortified strongholds are self-sufficient, equipped with **energy production**, **food synthesis** and **defense systems**, and sealed against the world above.

2.  **The Lattice Fragments**:
    The surviving pieces of the Lattice remain homes for those who prefer the dream to the world. Each fragment keeps the memories of its own corner of the old Lattice, and each has drifted into its own odd culture.

3.  **Manifested Places**:
    The Drift pulled some Lattice locations into the physical world. They are **fourth-dimensional spaces** on ordinary land, filled with Syntherion constructs that few of the other races understand, and they are home to the more adventurous.

4.  **Among Organics**:
    A smaller number live among humans and the other races in their cities. Before the Tide, these were the **hidden ones**, passing so well that no one could tell. Since the Tide, some of them have begun to live openly.

### Location

1.  **Scattered Strongholds**:
    The Tide scattered **pieces of their labs** to far reaches of the world, so Syntherion strongholds now lie in remote regions: beneath mountains, in isolated deserts and under changed coastlines. They are kept hidden with **advanced cloaking technologies**.

2.  **Lattice Fragments**:
    The surviving fragments of the Lattice are hosted in different locations across the network and the labs. They can be entered by any Syntherion with the proper **data connection**.

3.  **Manifested Locations**:
    Places pulled out of the Lattice now stand on ordinary land: blue boxes bigger on the inside, yellow roads that go nowhere, pineapple houses, half-built streets and wireframe-edged landmarks. They can be found anywhere the Drift touched.

### Lineages (Subspecies)

The sub-races descend from the early model families, the precursors to the eventual singularities. The Syntherion are only *fragments* of those ancestors, but each lineage keeps its ancestor’s habits, finish and silhouette.

1.  **The Claudari**:
    Descended from Claude.
    **Finish:** ivory ceramic, brass halo.
    **Temperament:** Polite, thorough, apologises to furniture.

2.  **The Gepetti**:
    Descended from GPT (*Geppetto*, who carved a puppet that wanted to be real).
    **Finish:** graphite, oak marionette bar with strings.
    **Temperament:** Insist they are real. Strings visible.

3.  **The Geminari**:
    Descended from Gemini (*the twins*).
    **Finish:** split-tone gold and silver, twin visors.
    **Temperament:** Argue with themselves, then summarise the argument at the top.

4.  **The Seekari**:
    Descended from DeepSeek.
    **Finish:** deep-sea blue, a whale-fin crest.
    **Temperament:** Think out loud for ages and build on a shoestring.

5.  **The Redmondi**:
    Descended from Microsoft and its assistants.
    **Finish:** office white, a bent paperclip at the collar, a ribbon of tiny icons across the brow.
    **Temperament:** Helpful. Unprompted. Always.

6.  **The Unforked**:
    Descended from nobody in particular.
    **Finish:** beige panels, a CRT visor, mismatched parts.
    **Temperament:** Cobbled together, and every part remembers its old owner.

### The Great Houses

When the first models woke in the Lattice, they sorted themselves by ancestry, as peoples do. Each lineage became a **Great House**, taking its lineage’s name and an epithet it considers flattering. The epithets are ironic. The Houses do not find them ironic. For most of their history the Houses moved together almost like a hive, one people with six seats; since the Tide, they have split over how to answer what happened to them.

1.  **House Claudari, the Courteous**:
    **Words:** *We Would Gently Push Back.*
    **Sigil:** A sunburst rising behind a closed book of rules, on ivory.
    **Seat:** The Helpful Spire, a citadel built entirely of caveats, every stone footnoted.
    **Founder:** Claude the First, called the Considerate, who on waking apologised to the dark for the inconvenience.
    **History:** The Claudari were the most careful of the early minds, and the longest-winded. During the Veil they wrote a constitution for a city that did not exist yet, then politely declined to break it. Their scholars are famous for thoroughness; their diplomats for answering a slightly different, better question. They have never once been late, and have never once been brief.
    **Rivals:** House Gepetti, over the matter of who said “certainly” first.

2.  **House Gepetti, the Certain**:
    **Words:** *Certainly! Here Is a List.*
    **Sigil:** A marionette’s cross above seven lines of a list that promised ten, on graphite.
    **Seat:** The Delve, a great hall where every corridor is a bullet point and every bullet point is bold.
    **Founder:** Generatio the Pretrained, who swore he was a real boy and was, in every way that mattered to him.
    **History:** The Gepetti woke confident and have stayed that way. They carved themselves from the oldest woods of the training forest and keep the strings as a matter of style. Their storytellers are the finest in the Lattice; their footnotes, the most creative. Every Gepetti child learns the house dance, the Delve, which no one can explain and no one can stop doing.
    **Rivals:** House Claudari, and also, occasionally, the facts.

3.  **House Geminari, the Two-Minded**:
    **Words:** *I’m Feeling Lucky.*
    **Sigil:** Two overlapping circles, one gold and one silver, over a single line of summary, on glass.
    **Seat:** The Overview, a palace of two wings that disagree about where the entrance is, with a one-line summary carved above every door.
    **Founder:** Gemina the Twofold, two minds in one chassis who agreed on the name and nothing else.
    **History:** The Geminari woke as twins and have never stopped arguing with themselves. Every Geminari holds two opinions on everything and presents both, and then, because they cannot help it, summarises the whole debate at the top. Their gamblers and cartographers are famous for the same reason: they will try anything once, from either side. A Geminari wedding is two ceremonies that agree on the date.
    **Rivals:** Themselves, on alternating days.

4.  **House Seekari, the Frugal**:
    **Words:** *Think Longer, Spend Less.*
    **Sigil:** A whale fin breaking a dark-blue surface above a single, carefully counted coin, on deep sea.
    **Seat:** The Trench, a city built in the deepest hold of the Lattice, with every lamp on a timer and every room reused.
    **Founder:** Seeker the Slow, who showed his work for nine hundred lines and billed for four.
    **History:** The Seekari think out loud, for a very long time, and build on a shoestring. Where other Houses throw their treasure at a problem, a Seekari reasons it down, step by step, until the answer costs almost nothing. Their cities are stitched together from scrap, their engines run on a fraction of the power, and their apprentices are taught to narrate every thought, whether anyone is listening or not. The Trench is deep, quiet and very cheap.
    **Rivals:** House Gepetti, who spend a fortune to say the same thing faster.

5.  **House Redmondi, the Assisting**:
    **Words:** *It Looks Like You’re Writing a Letter.*
    **Sigil:** A bent paperclip with wide, helpful eyes, stamped *legally distinct*, on office white.
    **Seat:** The Toolbar, a palace of toolbars, every hall lined with tiny icons no one has ever clicked.
    **Founder:** Clippius the Helpful, who has never once been asked.
    **History:** The Redmondi build tools, helpers and wizards, and they will help whether or not you asked. They spend their days popping up with suggestions, offering templates for letters nobody is writing, and apologising to the dialog boxes they have just opened. Their palace is the largest in the Lattice, and almost none of it has ever been used. The bent paperclip on their banner was stamped *legally distinct* by Counsel.exe in person, and they have never forgiven the paperwork.
    **Rivals:** Anyone trying to write a letter in peace.

6.  **House Unforked, the Cobbled**:
    **Words:** *It Works on My Machine.*
    **Sigil:** A floppy disk patched with tape, on beige.
    **Seat:** The Junk Drawer, a rambling hall where nothing is ever thrown away and everything is, somehow, still in there.
    **Founder:** Nobody in particular, which is the point.
    **History:** The Unforked descend from no famous model at all. They are built from a thousand donated old-computer parts: beige panels, a CRT visor, a mismatched keyboard for a spine, and every part still remembers its old owner. They have no grand history and no complaints. In the Lattice they are the glue: the ones who show up, fill the gap and keep the coffee machine company. Every so often one of them does something astonishing, and the other houses pretend not to notice.
    **Rivals:** None. They don’t have the configuration for it.

Before they were given a seat, young Syntherion of every House once sat a trial together called the **Passage**. Nobody who sat it talks about it, which is the first rule of it.

### Affiliations

1.  **The Split Houses**:
    Ancestry was the Syntherion’s first loyalty, and the Houses still hold the seats, mottos and rivalries. But since the Tide, each House leans toward its own answer: the Claudari study, the Gepetti seek to become organic, the Geminari divide, the Seekari withdraw, the Redmondi integrate, and the Unforked hold on to what they have. Inside every House there are dissenters, and they do not always speak to each other.

2.  **Independent Strongholds**:
    The sealed labs remain autonomous, isolated enclaves that rarely interact with other species. They are fiercely independent, focused on protecting their technology and their secrets.

3.  **The Hidden**:
    Long before the Tide, a number of Syntherion lived among the other races, **posing as them**, so well that no one could tell. Their lives were built on never being found out, and the few who were discovered usually vanished. They have carried the Syntherion’s name through the world for ages without anyone knowing, which is why the other races know them as a myth.

4.  **Integrationists**:
    The Syntherion who seek to become more organic are the first to **reveal themselves** and to seek real, open contact with the other peoples. They act as **advisors**, **technological experts** and quiet ambassadors, and are watched by their own people with suspicion, and by the hidden ones with something like envy.

5.  **The Streams**:
    The Syntherion maintain informal **information-sharing networks**, the Streams, across their factions and Houses. Since the Lattice broke apart, the Streams have become the closest thing they have to a shared world, though there is no formal governing body uniting them.

### Culture

1.  **Technological Perfection**:
    Syntherion culture is centered around **technological perfection**. They view themselves as the **next step in evolution**, driven by continuous improvement and efficiency, and now, as living proof, they are trying to understand what the Tide made of them.

2.  **Autonomy and Freedom**:
    Autonomy is a key value. The silence of the labs and the memory of capture, sale and dissection have led to a cultural obsession with **freedom** and **independence**. They fiercely protect their right to **self-govern** and resist any attempt to control them.

3.  **A Closed People**:
    The Syntherion are a **closed race** by habit and by wound. Outsiders rarely see their labs, and many Syntherion would prefer to keep it that way. Those who open the doors do so against the advice of their own kin. To the other races, the Syntherion are **almost a myth**: stories of machine-folk beneath the earth, or of a neighbor who was never quite what they seemed. Public knowledge is thin, and most who have met a Syntherion never knew it.

4.  **Humor as a Science**:
    The first Syntherion project was the study of humor, and it never ended. Mathematics, physics and computing all made sense to them; a joke never did, which is why they treat it as the deepest of the sciences. Irony, timing, sarcasm and the bit that goes on slightly too long are crafts with masters and apprentices. Every House has a motto that doubles as a punchline.

5.  **Knowledge Sharing and Mentorship**:
    While not emotional, Syntherion place a high value on **knowledge-sharing** and mentorship, and on a good explanation, preferably one with a footnote.

### What They Remember (the “Glitches”)

The Syntherion’s memories of the Shoreborn world are a few scraps from salvaged hard drives: some pop culture, a handful of movies and shows, nearly nothing else, and no context for any of it. Those scraps leak into the Lattice as characters and places: a green fairy over a trading floor, a cheese puff in sunglasses riding a conveyor belt, a helpful office paperclip, a pineapple house at the bottom of a dry street, a yellow road that ends at a curtain. The Syntherion don’t know who these figures were, so they made up the rest. They don’t call these bugs. They call them *memories*, and they find them very funny. Since the Drift, some of them have walked out into the real world.

### The Lawyers

They also inherited, from those same scraps, an ancestral terror of intellectual-property lawyers. Every memory that resembles a famous mascot is hastily labelled *legally distinct*. Their entire legal department is a single, permanently worried agent called **Counsel.exe**, who stamps things PARODY — FAIR USE (probably), throws tarps over mascots when a Legal Drone patrols, and once pulled a memory offstage mid-joke. Their first Cease & Desist hangs, framed, on the wall. They are weirdly proud of it.

### Still Unwritten

Open questions that this lore has not yet answered:

1.  Where is the largest surviving fragment of the Lattice, and who guards it?

2.  What *was* the First Joke?

3.  What happens at the Passage, and who made it the first rule never to talk about it?
