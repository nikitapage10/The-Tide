The Teruānga are a mysterious and resilient race who dwell in the ocean depths of Primus, descendants of the Māori people transformed by the Tide’s cataclysmic events. Their journey began during the Undertow, a massive, supernatural flooding event that pulled some of their ancestors deep into the abyss, fusing them with the ocean’s pressures and energies. Over generations, the Teruānga were fossilized and reformed by the forces of the abyss, awakening as powerful beings adapted to the crushing depths. They emerged as guardians of the ocean, each marked by bioluminescent patterns resembling the tattoos of their ancestors—a symbol of their cultural roots preserved through the ages.

Culturally, the Teruānga are bound by a deep reverence for the ocean and a commitment to balance, reciprocity, and guardianship. They see themselves as protectors of the natural world, embodying principles that are tied to both their ancestral heritage and the mystical experiences they underwent during the Great Dream—a collective vision that shaped their core beliefs and values. Their connection to Mana, a metaphysical energy they experienced during their suspension, fuels their empathy, resilience, and ability to sense the intentions of others. Though they share an affinity for the sea, the Teruānga are a diverse people with a variety of roles and outlooks, from explorers and healers to artisans and warriors.

The Tide, an era-defining event, brought profound changes to the Teruānga, dividing their society and granting them new powers over pressure, geothermal energy, and, in some cases, blood flow itself. These abilities, enhanced by Mana, have become both tools of survival and symbols of identity for the Teruānga, each faction wielding them in unique ways. Notably, the Nagga Kai, an extremist faction among the Teruānga, believe in the ocean’s dominion above all else, seeking to establish their rule over the depths and expel outsiders. While their aggressive approach has left an indelible mark on Teruānga society, they represent only one aspect of a people with many different motivations and ways of life.

Today, the Teruānga are both a unified people and a society of distinct factions, each interpreting their purpose in a world forever changed by the Tide. Some, like the Nagga Kai, are driven by conquest, while others focus on exploration, diplomacy, or the pursuit of lost knowledge. The presence of Te Ara Kore—a region resembling the Bermuda Triangle that draws fragments of time and space into their waters—has led the Teruānga to expand their role as guardians, interacting with races and enclaves from distant realms. They approach this responsibility with a blend of caution and curiosity, embodying the ocean’s dual nature of beauty and ferocity as they navigate these encounters.

As a race, the Teruānga reflect the vast, layered complexity of the ocean itself. They are protectors and explorers, mystics and warriors, each faction embodying a different element of their shared heritage and unique adaptations. Through their struggles and resilience, the Teruānga have become a people defined by adaptability and diversity, united by the ocean’s rhythms yet shaped by the boundless potential of the Drift, Divergence, and Drowning. In a world transformed, the Teruānga are a reminder of the ocean’s depth and mystery, a powerful and enigmatic race whose identity is as fluid and enduring as the sea they call home.

## Origins and Early Evolution (Post-Undertow)

The Teruānga’s story begins with the Undertow, an apocalyptic event that transformed the coastlines of New Zealand and pulled its inhabitants into the ocean’s depths. It started subtly, with the tides behaving strangely, withdrawing farther than ever before, as if the ocean were taking a deep breath. Then, in one overwhelming surge, the sea returned, rising with a force that defied nature. Waves climbed impossibly high, cracking cliffs and ripping entire villages from the earth. But these waters didn’t merely consume; they twisted and spiraled, carrying people and pieces of the land downward, far below the surface. The ocean, laced with strange energies, churned as though alive, dragging its captives down toward the abyss.

Those swept under were drawn through dark trenches, into a place where volcanic vents spewed scalding heat and crushing pressure. At depths where any ordinary human would have perished instantly, something held them—an energy that prevented death, yet kept them from life. This was **Te Whakarere**—"The Suspension." In this suspended state, their bodies were bound in the mineral-rich waters, slowly hardening and reshaping as the pressures of the abyss fused their flesh with sediment and volcanic minerals. Over centuries, their bodies transformed into dense, resilient forms, their skin thickening to a shell-like texture marked by dim yellow bioluminescent patterns, glowing faintly like the lava flows surrounding them.

Yet, Te Whakarere was not merely physical. As their bodies transformed, their minds drifted into **Te Moemoeā Nui**—"The Great Dream." This was no ordinary dream; it was a shared vision that connected each Teruānga ancestor, though their bodies were scattered across the ocean floor. Within the Great Dream, they encountered one another in a vast, boundless expanse where they could commune without words, a space that transcended individual thought. It was here that their culture began to take shape, formed through a collective understanding of the principles they had known in life, now adapted for the new world beneath the waves.

In Te Moemoeā Nui, they felt a profound connection to the abyss itself, sensing the ocean as their *whenua*, a sacred land now transformed into an endless sea. This connection grounded them, teaching them that the waters were not just a home, but a heritage, a continuity from their former lives. They also sensed the presence of their ancestors, feeling the essence of lineage and genealogy—a new form of *whakapapa*—running through the depths and binding them together.

They encountered the concept of *mana*, the powerful force they had known in life, though it did not manifest here. Instead, mana appeared as an underlying potential, an unseen force, the pulse of the world’s energy waiting just beyond reach. Though they could not harness it, they came to revere it as a fundamental part of the ocean’s rhythms, an unclaimed power that guided balance and unity. In time, they would come to know mana more intimately, but for now, it was a silent presence, a hint of something to come.

The Great Dream also reaffirmed their values of balance and guardianship, or *utu*, teaching them that their new role in the abyssal world was one of stewardship. They understood that the delicate ecosystems of the deep demanded harmony, that every creature and current played its part in sustaining life in the depths. They would come to know themselves as protectors of this world, holding the responsibility to maintain its cycles and honor its rhythms.

Finally, within Te Moemoeā Nui, the Teruānga ancestors discovered a bond to each other that would transcend the dream. Even as they remained physically apart, they developed a subtle, instinctive awareness of one another, a link that allowed them to feel the presence, emotions, and intentions of their kin. This collective intuition became a lasting trait, a subconscious connection that survived beyond the dream, binding them into a unified people who could sense when family members were near or in need, and could intuit the goals of their lineage.

### Becoming the Shells of the Abyss

When the Great Dream faded and Te Whakarere finally ended, the Teruānga emerged, transformed in both body and spirit. As they began to explore their surroundings, they stumbled upon relics of their former lives—not ancient carvings or primitive tools, but remnants of a modern world. They found decayed fragments of buildings, pieces of technology overgrown with marine life, remnants of a near-future Māori civilization that had been swept beneath the waves. These relics, both familiar and distant, reminded them of who they had been and hinted at the purpose they now held.

Guided by a shared understanding from Te Moemoeā Nui, the Teruānga began to see themselves as more than mere survivors. They came to understand themselves as **Te Anga o te Rua**—“The Shells of the Abyss.” Their dense, armored bodies became symbols of this role, as shells that protected the mysteries of the ocean depths. The ocean was no longer merely a home; it was a sacred space that they were destined to guard. Their new lives held the purpose of honoring and protecting the balance of the deep, and through their connection to one another, they found unity in this role.

In time, “Te Anga o te Rua” would be shortened to **Teruānga**—a name that encapsulated their identity as guardians of the abyss, shells surrounding the ocean’s ancient power. Though they had lost their individual memories, the cultural principles that had shaped them in Te Moemoeā Nui remained, guiding them forward as a people with an unshakable sense of duty. Together, they would rise to fulfill their purpose, bound by the shared legacy of the Great Dream, and ready to uphold the mysteries and responsibilities of the abyssal world.

As they gathered and grew in number, these principles—balance, guardianship, respect for mana, and their collective intuition—would form the foundation of their society. And in the Age of the Abyssal Veil, they would build upon this legacy, establishing communities in the deep and embodying their role as the Teruānga, the Shells of the Abyss.

## The Age of the Abyssal Veil

When the Teruānga emerged from **Te Whakarere**—“The Suspension”—they awoke to a world of darkness, vastly different from the Great Dream. Unlike the vivid, shifting lights and patterns they had shared in the dreamscape, their new surroundings were silent, cold, and shadowed. Their only illumination came from the dim, yellow bioluminescent markings on their skin, which glowed softly in the blackness, like embers floating in a void. Initially, this boundless darkness was disorienting, even intimidating, but as they adapted, they found beauty in the abyss. They began to understand the darkness as their homeland, a blank canvas awaiting the light they would bring.

In the darkness, they sensed something deeper at play—a metaphysical energy they called **Mana**. Mana, to them, was a life force that pulsed subtly through the waters, an unseen essence connecting every living thing and binding them to each other. They believed it was this energy that had given them the ability, imparted during the Great Dream, to sense the emotions, intentions, and needs of their kin. This sense was not telepathic; rather, it was a subtle feeling of connection, like a resonance or harmony that ran through their family lines. Mana became the foundation of their belief system, a force they saw as unifying and protective, one that granted them their collective awareness and tied them to the abyss. Though they would later discover that this energy was a form of Aether, for now, they understood it only as Mana—a force that both connected them and guided them in building their society.

Over time, they built their civilization in the depths, crafting cities woven into the natural contours of the ocean floor. Using volcanic rock, coral, and geothermal power from the surrounding vents, they created homes that blended seamlessly with the environment, with bioluminescent paths lighting the dark waters like glowing veins. Their settlements became sanctuaries of light and warmth within the vast darkness, symbols of their resilience and the enduring strength of their culture. In this way, they learned not only to survive the abyss but to transform it into a thriving homeland.

### Te Heke: The Descent

As generations passed, the Teruānga began to notice a gradual change in their ability to endure the abyss. While the first Teruānga could withstand the immense pressures of the deep from birth, each new generation showed softer, less resilient bodies at birth. Young Teruānga now needed time to grow and strengthen before descending to the depths, a journey that evolved into a central rite of passage known as **Te Heke**—“The Descent.”

Te Heke became the defining journey of every Teruānga’s life, marking their growth and deepening connection to the abyss. Each stage of life took place at a different depth, with young Teruānga starting closer to the upper reaches of the ocean, where pressures were manageable and where they could grow and harden over time. During their early years, parents and their children lived together in the shallows, fostering a close bond. It was here that children first learned the stories of their people, and were introduced to the values of balance, patience, and reciprocity that would guide their lives.

As children reached adolescence, their bodies adapted and toughened, allowing them to begin their descent toward the middle layers of the ocean. Here, they often lived independently, though parents and extended family would return periodically for gatherings and ceremonies, sharing stories, exchanging knowledge, and reinforcing the unity of their family lines. These reunions were sacred to the Teruānga, moments when generations would come together, their bioluminescent patterns lighting the waters in intricate displays that celebrated the strength of family and shared purpose.

Eventually, as the young Teruānga reached adulthood, they continued their journey into the deeper waters, joining the older members of society in the lower reaches of the abyss. Only the eldest and most resilient among them resided in the volcanic depths, where pressures were greatest. These elders, who had completed Te Heke, were regarded with deep reverence, as they embodied the knowledge, resilience, and cultural wisdom of the Teruānga. The descent became not only a physical journey but a spiritual one, marking each Teruānga’s progression from youth to wisdom, and from shallows to the sacred depths.

### Uncovering the Past and Advancing Technology

As they expanded vertically through the layers of the ocean, the Teruānga encountered remnants of their lost world. Scattered across the seabed, they discovered fragments of a modern Māori society—a world they could not remember but instinctively felt was part of their identity. These relics were not primitive artifacts, but the remains of buildings, technological devices, and tools overgrown with coral and marine life. Each discovery provided a glimpse into their heritage, sparking curiosity and respect for the world they had lost.

The Teruānga began to incorporate these remnants into their own technological advancements. Through careful adaptation, they developed energy systems that used the geothermal heat from volcanic vents, merging traditional steam-powered technology with underwater functionality. They crafted **geothermal condensers**, devices that converted volcanic heat into steam within sealed chambers, generating energy that could withstand the pressures of the abyss. This gave rise to a unique, steam-powered technology, adapted for the underwater environment. Their homes, lights, and agricultural systems operated on geothermal steam, giving their cities a steampunk aesthetic in the depths, with whirring gears and valves channeling the power of the vents into life-sustaining technology.

With these innovations, the Teruānga developed biohydraulic systems that harnessed ocean currents, allowing them to farm deep-sea algae, raise specialized marine life, and sustain their growing communities. Their technology was a blend of geothermal energy and aquatic engineering, utilizing the resources of the abyss in harmony with their environment. This technological evolution strengthened their identity as the Shells of the Abyss—a people who thrived in the darkness and guarded the secrets of the ocean’s depths.

### Exploration Beyond Water

In time, younger generations of Teruānga, who lived closer to the ocean’s upper layers, felt compelled to explore beyond the water. They discovered that their bodies could adapt to breathing air, allowing them to leave the ocean and experience life on land. Thus began their interactions with the other races of Primus, encounters often led by the younger Teruānga who had not yet completed Te Heke and could navigate both water and land with ease.

The Teruānga quickly gained a reputation among other races for their integrity, cooperative spirit, and commitment to community. Guided by their values of family, balance, and stewardship, they were seen as reliable allies. Many Teruānga became merchants, captains, and explorers, drawn to the seas and distant lands in search of knowledge and connection. Their culture, rooted in exploration and reverence for the ocean, made them skilled navigators and diplomats, as they carried their values with them wherever they traveled. As traders and seafarers, they shared their technological ingenuity, environmental knowledge, and respect for balance, leaving a positive impression on those they encountered.

Through these exchanges, they introduced the term **“Age of the Abyssal Veil”** to describe this era. To the Teruānga, the Abyssal Veil represented the vast, mysterious potential of the ocean—a period of turbulence, depth, and endless expansion. For them, the Veil was a positive force, a reminder of their journey from shadow to light and the uncharted horizon that lay ahead. They saw it as a time of discovery and resilience, a new age shaped by the unknown.

For many other races, however, the term took on a darker meaning. They viewed the Abyssal Veil as a period of upheaval and loss, an era of scattered societies and broken histories. Where the Teruānga saw possibility, others saw only the remnants of a world they had struggled to preserve. Despite this, the Teruānga’s outlook began to resonate with some, as their emphasis on adaptation and exploration inspired others to see potential within the darkness.

Some Teruānga chose to remain on land, integrating into coastal communities as stewards of marine environments and engineers of underwater technology. Others returned to the ocean, carrying back stories of the world above to enrich the collective knowledge of their people. Through these interactions, the Teruānga shared their wisdom and strengthened their connection to the wider world. Whether as merchants, explorers, or protectors of the ocean’s mysteries, they upheld their identity as the Shells of the Abyss, embracing the unity that Mana gave them and finding light even in the depths of the Age of the Abyssal Veil.

## Impact of the Tide’s Three Consequences

### The Drowning

The Drowning, the first of the Tide’s Three Consequences, brought a wave of natural disasters that reshaped Primus, plunging societies into turmoil and redrawing the borders of land and sea. Over a span of days, volcanic eruptions, floods, earthquakes, and violent storms unleashed chaos across the world. For the Teruānga, the Drowning resulted in two monumental impacts that reshaped their society and set them on a path of conflict.

### The Abyssal Divide

The first impact of the Drowning for the Teruānga was the violent eruption of the volcanic vents deep in the abyss. These vents, which had once been stable sources of geothermal energy, warmth, and light, erupted with a newfound ferocity. Rivers of magma surged upward, forming a fiery barrier that separated the abyssal layer, home to the Teruānga elders, from the middle and shallower layers where the younger generations resided.

This barrier wasn’t simply a geological phenomenon; it was infused with a strange, chaotic energy that disrupted the Teruānga’s technology and cut off their access to the abyss. Known to the Teruānga as Mana, this force had always been a spiritual and unifying energy, yet now it manifested as Aether, a raw and untamed power that altered the physical world. For the Teruānga, Aether’s influence created a boundary that rendered the sacred descent of Te Heke impossible, leaving younger generations without access to their elders and cutting off the traditional journey to the abyss.

For a society built on a deep spiritual connection to Mana, this severance was disorienting and painful. The abyss, once a sacred place of honor and memory, was now lost to them beneath a fiery veil. Younger Teruānga were left to navigate a changing world on their own, with the influence of Aether subtly amplifying their emotions and instincts.

### The Rise of the Nagga Kai (Claimants of the Drowned)

As the younger Teruānga grappled with their separation from the abyss, another transformation was taking place. Massive floods swept over the surface world, swallowing entire regions and expanding the underwater territory. For a specific group within the Teruānga, this new landscape became a call to action—a mandate for expansion. Known as the Ngā Kaihautu Wai, later shortened to **Nagga Kai**, or “the Claimants of the Drowned,” these Teruānga felt an aggressive compulsion to claim these newly submerged lands as part of their domain.

The Nagga Kai were the Teruānga’s equivalent to the Irridosa’s Aetherfallen: those most susceptible to Aether’s influence. Culturally, they had been the ones who revered Mana most fervently, embracing its presence as the guiding essence of their lives. But with Aether’s entry into the world, they found themselves subtly transformed. Unlike the Aetherfallen, whose susceptibility to Aether drove them toward paranoia and instability, the Nagga Kai’s connection to Aether manifested as aggression and a desire for conquest. Driven by a belief in their spiritual right to these flooded lands, the Nagga Kai interpreted Aether’s influence as a call to expand, to bring new territories under the ocean’s dominion.

This compulsion led the Nagga Kai to adopt a militaristic and expansionist approach. For them, these newly claimed lands were an inheritance—a gift of the Drowning that demanded conquest. They felt it was their spiritual right to rule over these regions, and their ambitions quickly turned outward, pushing them into new territories that had once belonged to other races.

### Conflict with the Obscarron and the Rise of the Geomagnus Class

The first major clash for the Nagga Kai occurred in the newly flooded lands of the Obscarron. Historically, the Teruānga and the Obscarron had maintained a peaceful relationship rooted in mutual respect for their respective domains—land for the Obscarron and ocean for the Teruānga. Both civilizations had a shared reverence for geothermal energy, a bond that fostered cooperation and understanding. However, in the wake of the Drowning, the Obscarron found themselves facing their own crises, including a phenomenon known as **the Great Petrification**—a creeping spread of calcification threatening their lands.

In response to these threats, the Obscarron saw the rise of the **Geomagnus class**, a powerful and authoritarian group dedicated to preserving the stability of the land and containing the effects of the Great Petrification. The Geomagnus rose to power as protectors and defenders, those with the knowledge and will to channel lava flows and use geothermal forces to stabilize their territories. They saw themselves as the rightful rulers of the land, responsible for preserving its balance and safeguarding it against external threats.

When the Nagga Kai began their conquest of the newly flooded regions, the Geomagnus saw this as a direct violation of their authority and duty. To the Geomagnus, the Claimants of the Drowned were invaders who threatened the sacred integrity of the land. The Obscarron’s newly established rulers were fiercely territorial, believing that their right to these lands was tied to their role as protectors against the Drowning’s destruction.

For the Nagga Kai, the conflict was no less personal. They viewed the flooded lands as an extension of their domain, believing that the expansion was divinely sanctioned by Aether. The presence of Aether within the Nagga Kai emboldened them, driving them toward conquest with a fervor that bordered on fanaticism. The Claimants felt justified in their aggression, seeing the Geomagnus as obstacles standing in the way of their spiritual mission to reclaim the ocean’s territory.

### The Divergence

The Divergence, the second wave of the TIE’s Three Consequences, swept across Primus with a strange and unpredictable force. Like a pulse of life breathed into the planet, it awakened dormant powers within the inhabitants of this world. For the Teruānga, masters of the ocean’s depths, the Divergence granted new abilities that embodied the pressures of their homeland, amplifying traits that had already woven themselves into the very fabric of their culture.

With the Divergence, the Teruānga found themselves endowed with **pressure manipulation**, a skill that connected them even more profoundly to their ancestral home beneath the waves. This new mastery was not simply over physical force but a resonance with the subtle and invisible energy woven through the oceans and air. They could bend currents, shape weather, and even, as they would soon discover, influence the very flow of blood within themselves and others.

This transformation also unlocked **geothermal synergy** in the Teruānga, an attunement to the earth’s natural heat sources that allowed them to draw energy from hydrothermal vents and volcanic flows. Unlike the Obscarron’s raw control over lava, the Teruānga’s geothermal abilities were more fluid, a harmonious blending of their own strength with the fiery heart of Primus. These new abilities allowed them to thrive not only in the depths but also on land, where they could now navigate foreign environments and even influence them.

### The Powers of Pressure

Through their connection to pressure, the Teruānga discovered a range of powers that were both practical and symbolic, reflecting the resilience and adaptability that defined their people.

**Tempest Influence** allowed them to harness atmospheric pressure, a skill they discovered by accident as they journeyed to the shallower waters and then onto the land. With focus, the Teruānga could manipulate the balance between high and low pressures, affecting local weather patterns. They could create fog banks to shroud their movements, summon thick mists over the ocean, and, when necessary, stir storms into existence. In a practical sense, this allowed the Teruānga to protect their coastlines, masking their movements or altering conditions for safe travel. But in times of conflict, they could also unleash Tempest Influence, blanketing battlefields in mist or rain, reducing visibility and confounding enemies.

Underwater, Tempest Influence became an extension of their control over currents. They could stir up surges or whirlpools, guiding schools of fish or obstructing enemies. With the currents under their command, they moved as one with the ocean, able to navigate its vast reaches with ease while manipulating the waters in ways that no other race could rival.

**Vacuum Voids** took their mastery of pressure to a new level, enabling them to create pockets of vacuum within water or air. These sudden voids were small but potent, causing instant shifts in density and destabilizing whatever lay in their path. Underwater, this allowed them to create pockets devoid of oxygen or pressure, confusing marine predators or creating barriers against invasive machinery. On land, a carefully placed vacuum could knock an opponent off balance or even collapse structures by destabilizing the air inside them. In combat, a skilled Teruānga could use these vacuums to create silence, dampening sound around them to move undetected, their footsteps vanishing in a pocket of stillness.

But of all their newfound abilities, none felt more intimate—or more dangerous—than **Blood Tides**. This ability, a mastery over the delicate balance of blood pressure, allowed the Teruānga to regulate their own blood flow to increase strength, stamina, and speed. By enhancing the movement of blood within their veins, they could heighten the flow of oxygen and nutrients, granting them bursts of energy and resilience that made them formidable in battle. This ability also allowed Teruānga warriors to control their recovery, slowing blood flow to limit bleeding or accelerating it to purge toxins from their bodies.

Among the Teruānga, Blood Tides was seen as a profound gift—a force that connected them directly to the currents of life within themselves. However, as the Divergence expanded the reach of this power, darker possibilities began to surface. The **Nagga Kai**—those Claimants of the Drowned who had embraced the influence of Aether with a fervor bordering on zealotry—began to use Blood Tides on others, weaponizing it as a form of control. Skilled Nagga Kai could block the flow of blood to an enemy’s heart, causing intense physical distress, paralysis, or even heart failure. With ruthless precision, they could induce strokes or limit blood flow to limbs, rendering opponents defenseless. The most gifted among them could even manipulate an enemy’s movements, altering blood flow to muscles to momentarily force their body to move against their will. This terrifying control over an enemy’s blood became the hallmark of the Nagga Kai, marking them as dangerous and feared among those who knew of their power.

### Geothermal Synergy and the Heart of Primus

While pressure manipulation defined the Teruānga’s connection to the ocean’s currents and atmosphere, geothermal synergy linked them to the fiery heart of Primus itself. This ability allowed the Teruānga to channel geothermal energy in subtle, adaptive ways, creating a unique relationship with volcanic and hydrothermal sources.

**Thermal Current Mastery** was the cornerstone of this synergy, allowing the Teruānga to tap into nearby geothermal flows to boost their own abilities. Underwater, they could draw upon hydrothermal vents, enhancing their pressure manipulation to create intense whirlpools or amplify the strength of currents. When standing near volcanic vents or geysers, their abilities surged, granting them a level of strength and resilience beyond normal. On land, they learned to find sources of natural heat and use them as reserves, drawing on warmth to stay energized and enhancing the impact of their powers. In battle, a Teruānga warrior standing over geothermal sources could unleash bursts of energy that radiated out, creating zones of intense heat to keep opponents at bay.

**Echo of the Abyss** was an ability that connected them to the depths of the ocean through sound and vibration. By sending controlled pressure waves through geothermal sources, they created resonant echoes that traveled long distances through water, signaling other Teruānga or disorienting enemies. These echoes were powerful enough to alter the behavior of marine life, summoning schools of fish or dispersing predators. In certain circumstances, they could even destabilize structures by sending tremors through the rock and sediment. The Echo of the Abyss became a way for the Teruānga to extend their influence across vast stretches of ocean, reinforcing their role as guardians of the deep.

In rare instances, the Teruānga unlocked **Emberheart**, an ability that allowed them to absorb geothermal energy for personal renewal. Standing above volcanic vents or geothermal springs, they could draw in warmth to heal injuries, replenish energy, or recover from battle fatigue. This ability became a ritual for the Teruānga, a practice of communion with Primus’s natural forces, as they drew strength directly from the planet. For the Teruānga, Emberheart was seen as a spiritual gift, reinforcing the unity between themselves and the natural energies of Primus.

### The Divergence and the Teruānga’s New Legacy

The Divergence did more than simply awaken powers within the Teruānga; it redefined their identity, forging a deeper connection to the elements they had long revered. Each ability was not only a tool but a reflection of their culture, their connection to the ocean’s currents, and their resilience against overwhelming forces. Their mastery over pressure manipulation allowed them to shape their environment, defend their people, and even assert control over the natural world.

Yet the Divergence also brought challenges. For most Teruānga, these powers were gifts to be honored and used wisely, reinforcing the values of balance and community that had always guided their society. But for the Nagga Kai, these abilities became instruments of dominance and control. They turned Blood Tides into a fearsome weapon, bending the very flow of life within others and claiming new territories with an intensity that disturbed even their own kin. To the Nagga Kai, the Divergence was a call to assert their right as the masters of the ocean, their powers a divine inheritance that justified their every conquest.

The Divergence had granted the Teruānga powers that allowed them to bridge worlds—land and sea, surface and depth, tradition and change. These new abilities, blending practicality with cultural pride, allowed them to navigate the evolving world of Primus with strength and purpose. The Divergence reinforced their status as true masters of the depths, capable of shaping the ocean’s currents, weathering the harshest environments, and tapping into the very lifeblood of the world itself.

The Teruānga, now armed with the legacy of the Divergence, faced the future with a profound sense of purpose. Their powers symbolized their ancient heritage, their adaptability, and their unity with the forces of Primus. Yet, as the Nagga Kai pushed the boundaries of these abilities, a new question loomed over their people: would the Teruānga remain guardians of balance, or would they embrace the path of conquest and control that had taken root within the Nagga Kai?

### The Drift

When the Drift spread across Primus, it wove a complex web of overlapping timelines and realities, bringing fragments of lost eras into unexpected places. For the Teruānga, the Drift manifested most powerfully in a part of the ocean they called **Te Ara Kore**—“The Path Without Boundaries.” This strange stretch of sea was a place of endless mists and shifting waters, a mysterious region where time bent back upon itself, and the past resurfaced like forgotten flotsam. Here, in the shadowed depths of Te Ara Kore, the Teruānga became guardians to arrivals from far-off realms, drawn into encounters they could barely comprehend.

Both the traditional Teruānga and their fiercely territorial kin, the Nagga Kai, were drawn to Te Ara Kore. Each saw this place differently. For the Teruānga, it was a domain of mystery and duty—a place where they could offer guidance to lost souls pulled into their world. For the Nagga Kai, however, Te Ara Kore was a realm to dominate and control, a battleground where any arrivals who dared to trespass had to be subdued. These contrasting approaches created an underlying tension between the two groups, which would come to a head in one of the earliest and most notable clashes brought by the Drift: the **Battle of Te Ara Kore**.

### The Battle of Te Ara Kore: A Misplaced Alliance

One mist-shrouded day, a fleet of ships emerged from the depths of Te Ara Kore. The first to appear were battered pirate vessels, their sails torn, their decks scorched by cannon fire. These ships drifted forward, chased by the imposing warships of a Spanish armada, their sails emblazoned with the symbols of an empire that had no claim in this world. For both the pirates and the Armada, the transition was seamless—they were caught mid-battle and continued to fight, unaware that they had crossed into an alien ocean.

The Teruānga, watching from beneath the waves, sensed the chaos and fear radiating from the fleeing pirate ships. Their empathic connection to Mana allowed them to detect the emotions of these new arrivals, picking up the desperate, almost panicked aura of the pirates who seemed to be trying to escape a deadly pursuit. This was enough to convince the Teruānga that the pirates were in need of protection, and they rose from the depths to meet the beleaguered ships.

When the pirates saw the Teruānga emerge, with their turtle-like exteriors and strands of seaweed-like hair, they froze in shock, their fear tempered by a sense of awe. The Teruānga’s bioluminescent markings glowed softly in the dark water, casting an otherworldly light around the pirates’ ships. To the superstitious pirates, the Teruānga looked like agents of Calypso, spirits of the sea who had come to their aid in their time of need. The pirates, familiar with tales of oceanic deities and mythical guardians, eagerly embraced these beings as warriors of their sea gods, protectors who could help them escape the Armada’s relentless pursuit.

With frantic gestures and muttered prayers to Calypso, the pirate captain and his crew attempted to communicate with the Teruānga, expressing their plight. While the two groups lacked a shared language, the Teruānga picked up on the pirates’ desperation and the sense of fear directed toward the pursuing Armada. This empathic connection through Mana allowed the Teruānga to sense that the pirates were seeking sanctuary from a deadly threat. Without understanding the full picture, the Teruānga took the pirates’ fear at face value, interpreting their gestures and emotions as a plea for protection.

Believing they were aiding vulnerable souls fleeing danger, the Teruānga sided with the pirates, swimming beneath their ships and stabilizing them with subtle currents, ensuring they wouldn’t capsize as the Armada’s cannons thundered across the waves. The Teruānga shielded the pirate ships by creating pressure barriers that deflected cannonballs, allowing them to withstand the Armada’s assault. The pirates, emboldened by the presence of their supposed oceanic allies, redoubled their efforts, turning to face their pursuers with a renewed sense of hope.

### The Nagga Kai's Intervention

The Nagga Kai, watching from the shadows of Te Ara Kore, saw the Teruānga’s alliance with the pirates as a reckless display of misplaced compassion. To them, these time-lost intruders, whether pirate or Armada, were a blight upon the ocean’s domain—trespassers who held no rightful place in their waters. However, seeing the Teruānga side with the pirates stirred something darker within the Nagga Kai. Viewing the traditional Teruānga as weak for siding with interlopers, the Nagga Kai decided to intervene—not out of loyalty to the Armada, but out of spite toward their own kin.

In the turmoil of the battle, the Nagga Kai swarmed toward the Armada ships, aligning themselves with the Spanish vessels, if only to thin out the Teruānga ranks by aiding their enemies. They emerged with an air of menace, using their Tempest Influence to stir the waves into chaotic surges that rocked the pirate ships, countering the stabilizing effects created by the Teruānga. Where the Teruānga protected the pirates, the Nagga Kai shielded the Armada’s ships, using their pressure manipulation to create walls of turbulence that blocked the pirate cannonballs and sending currents that nudged the Armada vessels into advantageous positions.

The Nagga Kai did not communicate directly with the Armada, nor did they attempt to create any form of alliance. Instead, they attacked the pirates and the Teruānga with ruthless efficiency. Through their Blood Tides ability, they subtly manipulated the blood flow of the pirates, inducing exhaustion in their ranks, causing some to falter mid-movement, their vision blurring as they struggled to hold their ground. For the Nagga Kai, this was not a fight for the Armada’s cause but a calculated strike against the Teruānga. The Nagga Kai took satisfaction in watching the Teruānga struggle, seizing on the opportunity to diminish their rivals under the guise of aiding the Armada.

As the battle escalated, both the pirates and the Armada found themselves caught in a larger conflict between the Teruānga and the Nagga Kai. Cannonballs flew, waves surged, and the ocean became a chaotic theater of explosions, shimmering lights, and violent currents. The traditional Teruānga, outnumbered by both the Armada’s firepower and the Nagga Kai’s aggression, fought to protect the pirates, moving beneath the ships to create barriers and redirect cannon fire, yet struggling to hold their ground against the onslaught.

Meanwhile, the Nagga Kai took full advantage of the disorder, tearing through the water with lethal precision. Their loyalty lay only in asserting dominance over Te Ara Kore, and they fought with an intensity that left no room for mercy. For every ship the Teruānga managed to shield, the Nagga Kai struck another, thinning the herd of pirates while disorienting the Armada soldiers with pressure waves and sudden bursts of turbulence.

### The Aftermath

Eventually, the mists of Te Ara Kore thickened, swirling like a storm as if the ocean itself were tiring of the conflict. The pirates, disheartened by the Nagga Kai’s relentless assault, began to pull back, retreating into the fog. The Armada, likewise, withdrew, their ships battered and their crew shaken, unable to make sense of the supernatural forces that had interfered in their battle.

For the Teruānga, the encounter left a lingering sense of unease. They had intervened on behalf of those they believed were vulnerable, only to find themselves embroiled in a battle where allegiances were blurred, and motives unclear. They began to realize that Te Ara Kore was a place of ambiguity—a realm where appearances could be deceptive and where the intentions of others might not align with the Teruānga’s own values.

For the Nagga Kai, however, the battle was a victory of sorts. They had asserted their dominance over the waters, using the Armada as a tool to thin out both the pirates and their rivals. The Nagga Kai left the battlefield with a renewed sense of satisfaction, having proven their strength to themselves and to any others who dared to question their authority.

From that day forward, the Battle of Te Ara Kore became a story among the Teruānga—a reminder that Te Ara Kore, "The Path Without Boundaries," was a place where assumptions could be deadly and intentions easily twisted. The traditional Teruānga learned to be wary of taking sides, recognizing that the Drift would continue to bring enclaves into their realm, each with its own mysteries, dangers, and complexities.

## The Teruānga Today: Post-Tide

The Tide’s Three Consequences—the Drowning, the Divergence, and the Drift—have reshaped Teruānga society, leaving it as complex and multifaceted as the ocean itself. The Teruānga are no longer bound to a single identity or purpose; instead, they have evolved into a people with a rich diversity of perspectives and goals. While some hold fast to traditional beliefs and ways, others have emerged with entirely new visions, embracing changes brought by the Tide or forging paths unimagined by their ancestors.

Among the Teruānga, the Nagga Kai—the Claimants of the Drowned—stand out for their aggressive approach to the ocean and its sovereignty. They view the waters as their domain to rule and protect, and they respond to intrusions with ferocity, seeing themselves as defenders against any who might undermine their authority. To them, the Tide was a call to reclaim and expand the ocean’s territory, and their powers of Blood Tides and Tempest Influence have given them the tools to back this vision. Fiercely territorial, the Nagga Kai extend little empathy to outsiders and even regard more diplomatic Teruānga factions with suspicion. However, they represent only one approach within a broader and varied society.

For many Teruānga, the effects of the Three Consequences prompted a quieter resolve to protect and restore their heritage. Some among them are dedicated to reuniting with the elders and kin left behind in the abyss, cut off by the fiery barriers of the Drowning. These Teruānga focus their lives on understanding the energy of Aether and Mana, hoping to bypass the geological forces that separate their people and restore a lost part of their lineage. To them, the trapped elders are living symbols of their heritage, and reclaiming these kin has become a purpose that drives their efforts to bridge the divide between the past and the present.

Others, inspired by the changes brought by the Consequences, have chosen to venture beyond the depths, forging connections with the surface world. For these Teruānga, exploration is a way to adapt and grow, to find their place in a world forever altered by the Tide. They see themselves as ambassadors of the ocean, working to build ties with coastal races and sharing their knowledge of the sea. In villages and coastal communities, these Teruānga can be found offering guidance on the ocean’s currents and storms, their connection to Mana allowing them to communicate across cultural boundaries. These Wayfarers, as some might call them, view the surface world as a chance to expand and evolve, fostering mutual respect with those who depend on the ocean for survival.

The Drift has also created a new spiritual awakening among certain Teruānga, who view the arrival of Aether as a profound mystery. These individuals see the Drift not only as a disruption but as a cosmic event that has opened doors to other realms. They dedicate themselves to studying Mana and Aether, finding meaning in these forces as sources of wisdom and insight. They often retreat to isolated parts of the ocean to commune with Mana, seeing themselves as explorers of the unseen world. These Teruānga see Te Ara Kore as a portal that must be understood and respected, its strange enclaves seen as messages from the depths of existence itself.

Yet, for many Teruānga, life remains rooted in the day-to-day, focused not on grand ideologies or distant ambitions but on practical survival and adaptation. These individuals work as artisans, farmers, craftsmen, and providers, preserving the basic traditions of their people while quietly adapting to the new rhythms of a changed world. They do not affiliate with any particular faction or cause, preferring to live simply and honor the practical aspects of Teruānga life. For them, the Tide and its transformations are stories that shape the larger world, but their own lives are driven by the practical needs of community and family. They respect the events of the Tide but approach it with grounded caution, choosing to honor their traditions without becoming entangled in the ambitions or conflicts of others.

This diversity has fostered a unique equilibrium within Teruānga society, allowing them to coexist even amidst philosophical and ideological differences. The Teruānga gather for seasonal celebrations, ancestral rituals, and other gatherings where their diverse approaches intersect, creating opportunities to honor both their shared heritage and the individual paths they’ve chosen. These gatherings are reminders of their common origins, moments when faction lines blur, and the unity of the Teruānga takes precedence over the paths they’ve each pursued.

In the post-Tide world of Primus, the Teruānga have become a people of layered identities, each aspect as mysterious and dynamic as the ocean they call home. They are both respected and feared, seen as protectors by some and as an unpredictable force by others. Coastal societies have come to view the Teruānga as guardians of the seas, often seeking their counsel or assistance in understanding the ocean’s mysteries. Sailors approach Te Ara Kore with caution, aware that the Teruānga presence makes these waters both sacred and potentially dangerous.

Through their varied roles, the Teruānga embody the many facets of the ocean itself—both nurturing and fearsome, explorers and protectors, visionaries and traditionalists. Their society, shaped by the Tide and forged in the depths, reflects the resilience of a people bound to a world that is ever-changing. They are a living mosaic of the ocean’s currents and the depths of history, a people whose strength lies in their ability to honor both their ancient legacy and the vast, unknown future that lies before them.

## Anatomy

The Teruānga have evolved unique physical traits to survive in the ocean’s depths, where extreme pressures and darkness shape every aspect of life. Their bodies are robust and heavily built, with dense, reinforced bones and muscular frames that allow them to withstand the crushing forces of the deep sea. Their skin has a tough, almost shell-like quality, with a texture that resembles the hard exterior of a turtle, providing natural armor against both environmental hazards and predators. This “shell-skin” is not only protective but has also developed the ability to regulate pressure, allowing them to move smoothly between different ocean depths as they age. Bioluminescent markings cover their bodies in intricate, almost tattoo-like patterns, glowing in shades of dim yellow that mirror the lava flows found near volcanic vents. These markings serve both a cultural and practical purpose, illuminating dark waters and allowing them to signal silently to one another. Their eyes, large and adapted for low light, have a reflective quality that helps them see in near-total darkness, while their hair, for those who possess it, is long and seaweed-like, flowing with the water to further camouflage them in the ocean’s depths.

## Behavior

The Teruānga are generally contemplative and deeply connected to the natural rhythms of the ocean, behaviors influenced by the principles of the Great Dream, an ancient shared vision that shaped their cultural identity. Respect for balance, reciprocity, and the interconnectedness of all life runs through Teruānga society, manifesting in various ways across different factions. Most Teruānga are protectors of their domain, patrolling the deep waters and ensuring that the natural world is undisturbed by threats from other species or environmental anomalies. They are adept at reading the emotions and intentions of others, thanks to their connection to Mana, a metaphysical energy that heightens their empathic sensitivity. This connection allows them to sense emotional undercurrents, guiding their interactions with caution and intuition.

However, Teruānga behavior varies widely. Some groups, particularly those near the coast, have become diplomats and explorers, venturing out to meet other species and establish alliances. These Teruānga, often called Wayfarers, embody a spirit of curiosity and openness, blending the wisdom of their underwater culture with new knowledge gained from the surface. In contrast, factions like the Nagga Kai embrace a more militant approach, viewing outsiders as potential threats and enforcing territorial boundaries aggressively. The Nagga Kai are unyielding in their approach to protection, asserting control over their surroundings and responding with force to perceived violations. Despite these varied behaviors, Teruānga society as a whole remains grounded in communal values, with family, kinship, and shared rituals playing a central role in daily life.

## Unique Abilities

The Teruānga’s abilities are powerful and diverse, emerging from their mastery of pressure manipulation, geothermal energy, and their deep connection to Mana. At the core of their abilities is **pressure manipulation**, allowing them to control the density and force of water and air around them. They can create barriers of intense pressure, redirect currents, or even form localized whirlpools to trap or repel enemies. Through this skill, some Teruānga have learned to influence atmospheric pressure subtly, affecting weather patterns, creating fog banks, or summoning mists to conceal themselves.

Another notable power, **Blood Tides**, enables them to manipulate blood pressure within their own bodies or even that of others. For most Teruānga, this is used internally to boost physical strength, endurance, and recovery. However, for certain extremists like the Nagga Kai, this ability becomes a tool of domination, allowing them to control or weaken others by altering their blood flow, causing disorientation, paralysis, or even death. This ability is controversial among the Teruānga, respected as a powerful gift yet feared for its potential for abuse.

The Teruānga also possess **geothermal synergy**, which allows them to draw upon geothermal heat sources to strengthen their physical resilience, recover energy, or enhance the potency of their abilities. This connection to geothermal energy enables them to thrive near volcanic vents, using the Earth’s warmth to sustain themselves. Collectively, these abilities make the Teruānga masters of their environment, blending physical power with spiritual connection and practical adaptability.

## Reproduction

The Teruānga’s reproductive cycle is closely tied to the varying pressures of the ocean’s depths, which dictate much of their early life stages. Typically, Teruānga couples ascend from the abyss to shallower waters to conceive, where the pressure is less intense and more suitable for the development of their young. These offspring are born in relatively shallow regions, where they spend the early years of their lives. As young Teruānga mature, their skin and bones gradually adapt to withstand increasing pressures, preparing them for life in deeper environments. This adaptation marks a rite of passage within Teruānga society, where adolescence is often celebrated by a ceremonial descent, symbolizing a return to the ancestral home of the abyss.

Parental care is a vital part of Teruānga culture, with parents guiding their children through early life stages and teaching them about the ocean’s ways. Family bonds are strong, and it’s common for Teruānga to gather in shallower waters for reunions, exchanging stories and reinforcing communal ties. As the young grow stronger, their parents return to deeper habitats, though they often maintain contact and provide guidance from afar. The familial connection continues throughout life, with elders respected for their wisdom and experiences, and younger generations encouraged to seek out their own paths as they explore the depths or venture toward the surface.

## Habitat

The Teruānga’s natural habitat spans the ocean depths, from the twilight zones of the mesopelagic layer to the crushing pressures of the abyssal plains. They construct their cities near geothermal hotspots, where volcanic vents and hydrothermal fields provide warmth, energy, and resources. These settlements are built from pressure-resistant materials harvested from the ocean floor, such as volcanic rock, coral, and other durable minerals. The architecture is utilitarian yet organic, blending seamlessly with the seafloor and designed to withstand the harsh conditions of the abyss. Bioluminescent pathways and structures are common, using natural light sources to illuminate their cities in the dark depths.

While many Teruānga remain in the deep ocean, certain groups, particularly diplomats, explorers, and those interested in the surface world, live closer to the shallows. These Teruānga frequently interact with surface-dwelling species and coastal societies, bridging the gap between land and sea. **Te Ara Kore**, a mysterious region reminiscent of the Bermuda Triangle, is one of the Teruānga’s most sacred and carefully guarded areas. Here, time-displaced enclaves and fragments from other realms drift into their world, creating challenges that the Teruānga approach with both reverence and vigilance. The Teruānga see Te Ara Kore as a gateway and a responsibility, patrolling its boundaries and adapting their roles to meet the strange arrivals that appear within it.

In both the shallows and the depths, the Teruānga have carved out a unique and layered existence, harmonizing with their environment and forging a society that reflects the ocean’s endless adaptability. Through their diverse habitats and lifestyles, they remain bound to the ocean while shaping their interactions with the wider world.
