### Before the Undertow (B.U.)

*Note: The following historical details are largely unknown to the current inhabitants of Ilyr, pieced together from fragmented relics and speculative Teruāngan interpretations.*

**~2012 B.U.:** Across the globe, the **Shoreborn** nations begin documenting subtle but persistent rises in global temperatures. While vigorously debated and largely attributed to industrial greenhouse gas emissions and natural cycles by their scientists and populace, these environmental shifts were, in reality, the almost imperceptible tremors preceding the cosmic storm of the Undertow.

**~2042 B.U.:** Decades of environmental pressure, coupled with resource mismanagement and burgeoning populations, lead to significant socioeconomic strain. Diplomatic ties between the powerful Shoreborn nations fray, resource conflicts simmer, and societal inequalities deepen, creating a fragile global ecosystem ripe for disruption.

**~2069 B.U.:** Facing escalating internal strife, dwindling key resources, and the undeniable stress on their planetary environment, the Shoreborn nations embark on a desperate, unified surge of scientific research and technological development. Vast resources are poured into energy production, environmental engineering, artificial intelligence, and even theoretical physics, hoping innovation can engineer a way out of their mounting crises.

**~2093 B.U.:** This intense focus yields remarkable results, ushering in a brief, intense 'golden era' lasting just nine years. Advancements in automation, energy, and potentially even early meta-materials briefly alleviate some pressures, creating a facade of progress and renewed hope, though the underlying planetary instability remains unaddressed.

**~2102 B.U.:** The Shoreborn era ends cataclysmically with the full onset of **the Undertow**. Lasting approximately nine months, this devastating period involves extreme geological upheaval, unprecedented atmospheric phenomena, gravitational anomalies, and some minor incursions of energies from beyond known reality. The planet's surface is scoured, coastlines are redrawn as sea levels drastically rise, and the vast majority of the Shoreborn population perishes. Most of the survivors are irrevocably altered, their very biology and environment warped, setting the stage for the emergence of the Ilyrian races.

### Age of the Veil (A.V.)

Emerging from the global devastation of the Undertow, the survivors entered the long twilight of the Age of the Veil. Spanning roughly seven centuries, this era remains poorly understood, a vast stretch of time obscured by few archived records and the profound isolation of the nascent Ilyrian races. It was a period defined less by recorded events and more by slow, fundamental processes: biological transformation, cultural genesis, and the painstaking struggle to rebuild life in a world rendered alien and hostile.

The nature of existence during the Veil varied dramatically. For some groups, the Undertow's effects were immediate and brutal, forcing them into a desperate fight for survival amidst shattered landscapes and ecological collapse. Their connection to the Shoreborn past was severed almost instantly, leaving them to construct new realities from instinct and environmental necessity. For others, the transformation was a far slower, more insidious process. Races like the Teruānga or Obscarron spent vast portions of this era suspended in states of deep metamorphosis, their bodies gradually fusing with minerals, stone, or abyssal energies over generations, their consciousnesses perhaps drifting in collective dream-states or locked in evolutionary stasis. The very definition of "life" and "time" may have differed profoundly for these groups compared to those actively rebuilding on the surface.

Isolation was perhaps the most universal constant of the Veil. The Undertow had not only shattered civilizations but also erected immense physical barriers – newly formed mountain ranges, vast inland seas, irradiated zones, impenetrable forests, or crushing abyssal depths. Furthermore, the transformations themselves often created biological barriers; the radioactive aura of the Irridosai, the unique atmospheric needs of certain groups, or the emergence of entirely new predators made interaction perilous. Compounding this was the psychological trauma of the Undertow, likely fostering an overriding focus on internal group survival, discouraging outreach even if it were possible.

Consequently, the Age of the Veil largely resembled a planetary 'dark age.' Advanced Shoreborn technology was mostly lost or non-functional. Knowledge fragmented into myth and superstition. Progress was incredibly slow, driven by rediscovery, adaptation to new physiologies, and cautious experimentation within isolated pockets. The presence or absence of accessible B.U. relics created pockets of slightly faster or slower development, but overall, the era was marked by technological scarcity and cultural divergence. As the Veil drew towards its close, and the environmental or psychological barriers began to slowly lower, the emerging races stood on roughly equal footing technologically, poised for the shock and potential of rediscovering each other. The end of this long isolation, driven primarily by the outward push of races like the Teruānga, ignited a period of intense global interaction and change, finally bringing the obscured Age of the Veil to an end and ushering in the era of recorded history.

### Era of Verdancy (E.V.)

The Era of Verdancy represents the current span of recorded history on Ilyr, beginning after the Age of the Veil and lasting approximately 3200 years to the present day. It is defined by the gradual reconnection of the planet's diverse peoples and the complex tapestry of cooperation, conflict, and cultural exchange that followed. This era was inaugurated not by a decree, but by a chance encounter that rippled across the globe: the event known widely as the "First Ascent," though Teruāngan historians often prefer the term "Ascent of the Wild Ones" (as the former name implies it was the first time their people reached land, which is inaccurate). While the exact details vary in the telling across generations, the core elements of this pivotal story remain consistent:

It is said that five young Teruānga, nearing the age of their Te Heke descent, ventured too far while exploring the upper reefs. Caught by an unexpected and powerful current, they were carried far from their home waters, eventually surfacing near a large, unknown island dominated by towering cliffs – the homeland of the Irridosai. As they oriented themselves, fate intervened. They witnessed one of several small figures high on the cliffs lose their footing and plummet into the sea below. Driven by an instinct ingrained through their connection to *Mana* and the cultural value of balance, the Teruānga youths swam to the aid of the fallen figure – an Irridosai girl, rendered unconscious by the fall and now drowning.

They managed the difficult task of bringing her ashore and scaling the cliffs, returning her to her worried companions. The initial interaction was tense; the Irridosai youths were fearful of the large, shelled newcomers but also, according to some tellings, concerned that their own radioactive nature might harm these strange rescuers, prompting them to quickly retreat into the island's lush interior with their companion. The curious Teruānga followed, venturing into the vibrant, energetic landscape. It wasn't long before they were intercepted by Irridosai scouts.

The scouts, surprised by the Teruānga's appearance and even more so by their apparent immunity to the island's radiation (which usually sickened or killed outsiders), escorted the young explorers to their village. Brought before the clan elders, the situation remained uncertain until the Irridosai youths arrived and explained the rescue. Recognizing the benevolence of the Teruānga and noting their unique resilience, the elders offered hospitality, especially as a storm gathered. A night of rudimentary communication followed, an exchange of goodwill and curiosity more than language. In the morning, the Teruānga were sent off with supplies for their journey home.

Their return and the recounting of their tale – of the strange, pale people, the vibrant yet dangerous land, and the unexpected connection forged through rescue – ignited the exploratory spirit of the Teruānga. Soon, more organized expeditions sought out the Isle of the Irridosai, establishing a tentative contact that grew into a lasting partnership. This event, the First Ascent, became legendary, symbolizing the end of the deep isolation of the Age of the Veil and launching the Teruānga into their role as navigators and connectors of the newly emerging world, marking the dawn of the Era of Verdancy and shaping much of the shared history and terminology of Ilyr.

Fueled by this encounter, the Teruānga, natural masters of Ilyr's vast oceans, embarked on voyages that gradually mapped the post-Veil world. They encountered the Blightmourn in their harsh deserts, the Umbrasa in their mountain strongholds, the Nyth'rok in their northern territories, and others, acting as unintentional messengers and catalysts for interaction. Through trade, shared stories, and sometimes conflict, they disseminated knowledge, linguistic foundations, and cultural concepts, slowly weaving the disparate peoples of Ilyr back together. Their diligent record-keeping, preserved in the deep abyss, provides much of the framework for modern historical understanding and the very terminology used to describe the eras.

The subsequent millennia of this era saw not just reconnection, but remarkable progress. Freed from the constraints of the Veil and fueled by the unique talents and perspectives of each race, civilization flourished. Technology progressed at an astonishing rate, quickly surpassing the achievements of the pre-Undertow Shoreborn. Leveraging rediscovered B.U. principles, unique biological adaptations, and entirely new scientific paradigms born from their post-Undertow realities, the races of Ilyr built incredible societies. Massive, technologically integrated cities sprawled across continents and soared amongst the clouds, while far-reaching civilizations established intricate global networks. This advancement extended beyond the planet itself. Multiple nations, harnessing their combined ingenuity, re-established a significant presence in space, founding permanent colonies not only on Ilyr's moon but also on other planets within their solar system. Orbital habitats became commonplace, and robust interplanetary trade and travel connected these off-world settlements. Furthermore, just prior to The Tide, the most advanced factions were embarking on ambitious, fledgling expeditions aimed at interstellar colonization, pushing the boundaries of known space. Before the disruptions began, Ilyr stood at the center of a bustling, technologically sophisticated interplanetary civilization, a vibrant ecosystem of interconnected races and powerful factions reaching for the stars. However, this complex and advanced civilization proved fragile in the face of the next great upheaval: The Tide.

### The Tide

Occurring within the later centuries of the Era of Verdancy, The Tide represents not a single, sharp catastrophe like the Undertow, but a more prolonged and perhaps ongoing period of profound, multifaceted disruption. Its impact is understood through its **Three Consequences**, phenomena that swept across Ilyr, challenging the established order and forcing further evolution and adaptation upon its inhabitants.

The **Drowning** manifested as extreme geological and hydrological instability on a planetary scale: coastlines vanished beneath rising waters, earthquakes rent continents, volcanoes roared to life with renewed fury, and vast tracts of land were literally torn from the planet's surface, becoming the eerily beautiful and gravitationally anomalous Floating Mountains.

Concurrently or subsequently, the **Divergence** occurred – a poorly understood event, perhaps a wave of cosmic energy or a shift in underlying reality, that unlocked latent potential within the Ilyrian races. This amplified existing traits or granted entirely new abilities, often tied to fundamental forces like gravity, entropy, quantum mechanics, or electromagnetism, leading to rapid power shifts, the rise of new elites (like the Geomagnus), and further societal fragmentation.

Lastly, the **Drift** introduced spatiotemporal chaos, causing "enclaves" – fragments of other timelines, realities, or distant locations, including puzzling remnants seemingly tied to pre-Undertow Earth – to materialize inexplicably across Ilyr. These intrusions brought new peoples, strange technologies, and profound existential questions. The ultimate origin, scope, and conclusion (or continuation) of The Tide remain among the greatest mysteries facing the scholars and peoples of modern Ilyr, with theories ranging from cyclical planetary events to instabilities in the fabric of reality itself.

### Terminology

Understanding the chronicles of Ilyr involves grasping key terms, many standardized through early Teruānga exploration and now common across literate societies.

- **Ilyr (adj. Ilyrian):** The contemporary name for the planet inhabited by the modern races. This name replaced the ancient Shoreborn designation, "Earth," which is now virtually unknown outside of Drift-related contexts. Individuals or things originating from Ilyr are referred to as Ilyrian. The name comes from the Teruānga for "light that remains."

- **B.U. (Before the Undertow):** Refers to the era of recorded Shoreborn (human) history before the first great cataclysm. Much of this history is lost and serves primarily as a distant chronological benchmark for us, the reader, attempting to understand the origins of Ilyr and its peoples. Roughly analogous to the C.E. (Common Era) system used by the Shoreborn themselves.

- **A.V. (Age of the Veil / Age of the Abyssal Veil):** The approximately 700-year period following the Undertow and preceding the Era of Verdancy. It was defined by the often slow and isolated processes of racial transformation, the genesis of new cultures from the ashes of the old, significant technological regression (a 'dark age'), and near-total separation between emerging groups. Because comprehensive records are exceptionally rare, it remains an age "veiled" in obscurity for most. The Teruānga originated the term "Abyssal Veil" reflecting their own transformation within the deep ocean; "Age of the Veil" is the more common modern usage.

- **E.V. (Era of Verdancy):** The current era of recorded history, dating from the end of the widespread isolation of the A.V. (symbolically marked by the "First Ascent") and spanning roughly 3000 years to the present. This epoch is characterized by renewed inter-species contact, the development of distinct civilizations, significant technological advancement far surpassing the Shoreborn (including interplanetary colonization), recorded historical narratives, and the establishment of standardized timekeeping (though calendar systems may vary regionally). The more recent period of disruption known as The Tide falls within the latter part of this long era. The name "Verdancy" stems from the Teruānga explorers' first impressions of the lush, green surface world encountered after emerging from the ocean depths.

- **Shoreborn:** The retrospective term applied to the pre-Undertow human inhabitants of Ilyr. It originates from the Teruānga term *Whānauta*, meaning "born of the shore," likely reflecting the Teruānga's perspective upon first encountering sunken ruins related to the land-dwelling precursor civilization.

- **The Undertow:** The singular, catastrophic event (circa 2102 B.U.) that precipitated the collapse of the Shoreborn civilization, drastically reshaped the planet's geology and environment, and served as the catalyst for the biological and/or metaphysical transformations that created the diverse Ilyrian races.

- **The Tide:** The collective designation for the second major period of planetary upheaval, occurring within the modern Era of Verdancy. It is not a single event but characterized by its **Three Consequences**: the **Drowning** (widespread geological/hydrological disasters), the **Divergence** (amplification or emergence of unique racial abilities), and the **Drift** (appearance of spatiotemporal 'enclaves' from other times/places). Its fundamental nature is poorly understood, often discussed metaphorically and potentially linked to phenomena beyond conventional physics.
